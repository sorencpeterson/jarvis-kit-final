Week 1 · Audit & Stabilize

Checklist

See the system

  • Map your delivery flow end to end — Intake → Scope → Production → QA → Client review → Ship. Write the actual steps, not the ideal ones.
  • Mark every step that requires you personally. That list is your bottleneck inventory.
  • List your recurring deliverable types (report, ad creative, landing page, strategy doc) and rank them by volume.
  • Find the single step where work waits longest. That's your constraint — everything in Weeks 2–4 should relieve it.

Stop the bleeding

  • For your 3 worst-margin clients: decide to fix, reprice, or fire. Put a date on each decision.
  • Kill or delegate one thing this week that only you do and that doesn't actually need you.
  • Write your constraint in one sentence and pin it where you'll see it daily.
Done whenYou can name your #1 constraint in a single sentence, and you've removed at least one founder dependency from the flow.
You'll have: a one-page delivery flow map with every you-only bottleneck circled.

Week 2 · Delivery Systems

Checklist

Document the work

  • Write SOPs for your top 3 deliverables. Format each: Trigger → Steps → Ship-ready definition → Who reviews. One page max.
  • Define "ship-ready" for each — the objective checklist that means it's done right. This replaces your gut.
  • Record a 5-minute Loom for each SOP. People follow a video over a doc.

Build the pipeline

  • Stand up a stage-gated pipeline in your PM tool: Intake → Scoped → In Production → QA → Client Review → Shipped. Write the gate criteria — what must be true to move forward.
  • Create one QA checklist run before anything reaches a client. Assign a QA owner who isn't you.
  • Push this week's real work through the new pipeline. Fix what breaks in real time.
Done whenA deliverable shipped this week without passing through your hands — and it met standard.
You'll have: 3 written SOPs, 1 QA gate, and a visible pipeline your team works from.

$1–2MPick the 3 deliverables you touch most. Don't boil the ocean — those 3 are 80% of your time back.

Week 3 · Growth & Automation

Checklist

Growth on rails

  • Get every lead and client into one CRM with a clear stage. No deals living in your head or your inbox.
  • Define pipeline stages and the exit criteria for each — what objectively moves a deal forward.
  • Build one dashboard with the numbers that matter: pipeline value, delivery margin, utilization, cash. One screen, reviewed weekly.

Buy back the hours

  • List your team's top 5 repetitive manual tasks — reporting, onboarding, status updates, invoicing, scheduling.
  • Automate the top 3 — whatever buys back the most hours. Use what you already have (GHL, Zapier, Make, native tools). Done beats elegant.
  • Add one AI assist where it's safe and saves real time — first-draft reports, notes → action items, a QA pass. Document the prompt so it's repeatable.
Done whenYour weekly numbers come from a dashboard, not a scramble — and three manual tasks now run themselves.
You'll have: 1 trusted dashboard, 3 live automations, and 1 documented AI workflow.
By stage
< $1MDon't over-engineer. A clean spreadsheet dashboard plus two automations beats a CRM you won't maintain.
$5M+You have tools — you have an adoption and data-hygiene problem. Consolidate to one source of truth.

Week 4 · Cadence & Handoff

Checklist

Install the rhythm

  • Set a weekly operating cadence: a 30-min Monday priorities meeting + a short daily standup (live or async). Same agenda every time.
  • Set a monthly review: the numbers, what shipped, what's stuck, decisions needed.
  • Start a decision log — who decided what, when, and why — so decisions stop routing through you.

Hand it off — for real

  • Assign a named owner to every system from Weeks 2–3. A system without a human responsible is already dead.
  • Run the full week deliberately stepping back. Note what breaks — that's your month-2 punch list, not a reason to jump in.
  • Schedule a no-founder day: unreachable for delivery decisions. See what holds and what doesn't.
Done whenThe agency ran a full week — including a no-founder day — without a fire only you could put out.
You'll have: a written operating cadence, a decision log, and a named owner for every system.
By stage
$2–5MInstall the cadence with your leadership layer, then have them run it down to their teams.
$5M+Your job is to make your directors own the rhythm. If it still depends on you, you've delegated tasks, not ownership.

Why installs die — and what Day 30 looks like

Keep it alive

The five ways this dies

  • It lived in a doc nobody opened. Run real work through every system the same week you build it.
  • You kept rescuing. Every rescue retrains the team to wait for you. Let small things break.
  • Too much at once. One system at a time, in order. The constraint first.
  • No named owner. A system without a responsible human is already dead.
  • Perfect over shipped. A B+ SOP your team uses beats an A+ one they don't.

What good looks like at Day 30

  • You can take a week off and the work still ships.
  • New work no longer means more of you.
  • Quality is defined, owned, and repeatable.
  • Your numbers live on one screen.
  • You're working on the agency, not in it.
The whole game
Stop being the smartest bottleneck in the building.
You don't need more people. You need the work to run without you. That's what these 30 days install — and what every week you wait keeps costing you in capacity, margin, and your own time.
