# cross-brand-router

## When to use
A lead needs sorting before anyone writes to them: which brand, which offer, which voice,
warm or cold. Input: what you know about them (business type, size, how they came in,
what they said). Output: the lane call ([SECOND_BRAND] vs [OWNER_COMPANY] agency), the
offer to aim at, the voice register, and the one misroute trap to avoid. Both brands are
[OWNER]. The router keeps the message matched to the buyer so a local owner never gets an
agency pitch and an agency never gets talked to like a local business.

## The core split
| | **[SECOND_BRAND]** | **[OWNER_COMPANY] (agency lane)** |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer | Local business owner, direct client | Agency owner/founder, 3-20 people |
| They're buying | More customers / patients / jobs for THEIR business | Fulfillment for THEIR clients' sites |
| The offer | A site (Landing/Standard/Booking/White-Glove), Webfix, Speed-to-Lead, care | Agency first build [FIRST_BUILD] flat, then rate card, NDA |
| Motion | Warm beats cold, hard. Re-open vague. | Cold works here too. Deadlined, not shopping. |
| Voice | Competent contractor helping a local owner get more business | Operator to operator: pipeline, margin, invisibility |
| Where they came from | The 481 warm list (booked calls + repliers) | Cold agency drip, network, referrals |

## The routing test (three questions, in order)
1. **Whose customers are they trying to get?** Their own -> [SECOND_BRAND]. Their
   clients' -> [OWNER_COMPANY]. This is the single cleanest cut.
2. **Are they a business that serves the public, or a business that serves other
   businesses' web needs?** Medspa, plumber, salon, clinic -> local, No BS. Agency,
   studio, marketing shop -> agency lane.
3. **How did they come in?** The 481 warm list came in on [SECOND_BRAND] about growing
   their own business, default them local. The cold agency drip and network intros are
   the agency lane. If the source says local but the conversation reveals an agency,
   switch lanes mid-stream (see the trap below).

## Offer + voice by lane
**[SECOND_BRAND] (local).** Re-open vague, diagnose live, pivot to the SKU that fits:
Standard [STANDARD_SITE], Booking [ECOM_PRICE], White-Glove [WHITE_GLOVE] (medspa/men's-health dead sites),
Webfix [WEBFIX], Speed-to-Lead [SPEED_TO_LEAD], Landing [LANDING_PAGE]. Care converts at delivery. Voice: helping
a local owner stop losing customers. Anchor to a lost patient/job in their units. Niche
books: medspa.md, mens-health.md, plus local service.

**[OWNER_COMPANY] (agency).** Lead with the resell margin and invisibility. First build
[FIRST_BUILD] flat, then the rate card ([STANDARD_SITE] standard, hold that as the ongoing, never promise
[FIRST_BUILD] past build one, offers.md TODO). NDA standard, their brand on everything, never
touch their client. Voice: operator to operator, the receipt closes, not adjectives.
Niche book: agency-white-label.md. Skill: agency-partner-pitch.

## The misroute traps (each kills a deal)
- **Pitching the white-label web offer to a warm-list local owner.** The 481 came in
  about THEIR business, not about reselling websites. Pitch them a site for their
  practice, never "become my agency partner." This is the most common misroute.
- **Talking to an agency like a local business.** No "more customers in the door." An
  agency has customers, they have a fulfillment problem. Talk pipeline and margin.
- **Missing the switch.** A warm-list local call sometimes reveals the person is actually
  an agency owner, or an owner buried in delivery. That's a lane switch to white-label or
  COO framing, mid-call. Watch for it, then change scripts.
- **[a family member's site] is [OWNER]'s DAD's copywriting site.** Copy-only work is the dad's
  lane, never routed to [OWNER] as his business. If a lead only needs copy, that's a
  referral to the dad, not a [OWNER_COMPANY] SKU.

## Voice (hard rules)
No em-dashes or en-dashes, ever. Short sentences, 9-13 words. Contractions always. No
emojis. Numbers do the talking. The two voices differ in vocabulary, not in the rules:
local leads on customer/patient/job value, agency leads on pipeline/margin/markup/
invisibility. Banned in both: unlock, leverage, seamless, elevate, excited, circle back.
Full spec: `business-library/VOICE-SPEC.md`.

## True facts you may state
Six years doing this. Fractional COO who scaled a marketing agency [PRIOR_RESULT] per

fulfillment, and why a local owner trusts the marketing judgment. A retained medspa tox

clients, review counts, or guaranteed results.

## WORKED EXAMPLE 1 — a medspa owner (route: [SECOND_BRAND], local)
*Input: Renee, owner of a medspa. Came in on the warm list, booked a [SECOND_BRAND] call
6 months ago. Runs tox, filler, facials.*

**The route: [SECOND_BRAND], local direct client.**
- **Routing test.** Whose customers? Her own patients. What kind of business? Serves the
  public. Source? Warm list, [SECOND_BRAND]. Clean local route, all three agree.
- **Offer to aim at.** A site for her practice. If her presence is a Fresha/Vagaro page
  or dead, White-Glove [WHITE_GLOVE]. If she has a weak-but-real site, Booking [ECOM_PRICE]. Diagnose
  on the call before naming one. Care Growth+ [CARE_PREMIUM]/mo is the delivery attach, not the
  pitch.
- **Voice.** Competent contractor helping a clinical owner get more patients booked. Talk
  patient volume, no-shows, the 9pm booker. Never CTR, never funnels, she's clinical-
  first and reads jargon as amateur hour.
- **The trap to avoid.** Do NOT pitch her the white-label web offer. She's not an agency,
  she can't resell a site. She books patients. The offer is a site for HER, and the
  re-open is vague ("still looking to get more patients booked?"), not a web pitch.

Why: a warm-list medspa is the textbook [SECOND_BRAND] local lead. Every signal points
the same way, and the one live danger is accidentally running the agency white-label
script at her, which is why the trap is called out explicitly.

## WORKED EXAMPLE 2 — a 5-person agency (route: [OWNER_COMPANY], white-label)
*Input: Sasha, founder of a 5-person marketing agency. Replied to the cold agency drip
asking how white-label overflow works. Their site shows client work but no dev
capability.*

**The route: [OWNER_COMPANY], agency white-label lane.**
- **Routing test.** Whose customers? Her clients'. What kind of business? Serves other
  businesses' marketing. Source? Cold agency drip. Clean agency route.
- **Offer to aim at.** Agency first build [FIRST_BUILD] flat, the proving rate, first order only.
  Then the rate card: [STANDARD_SITE] standard, [LANDING_PAGE] landing, [ECOM_PRICE] booking. Never promise [FIRST_BUILD]
  past build one. NDA standard. Frame every number from HER side: she bills $3,000-5,000,
  keeps the spread.
- **Voice.** Operator to operator. Pipeline, fulfillment, margin, markup, invisibility.
  The receipt closes: handed off Monday, client-ready Thursday. The COO story, one
  sentence. Never "more customers in the door," she already has customers.
- **The trap to avoid.** Don't talk to her like a local business, and don't undersell the
  invisibility. Her deepest fear is a vendor who goes around her to her client. Say NDA
  standard and "your brand on everything, I never touch your client" before she asks.

Why: a cold-drip agency asking about overflow is the textbook [OWNER_COMPANY] lead, and
cold is fine in this lane specifically. The margin math and invisibility rules carry it,
and the trap here is the mirror of example 1: treating an agency like a local owner
guts the pitch.
