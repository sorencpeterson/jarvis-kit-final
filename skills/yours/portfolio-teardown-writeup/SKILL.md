# portfolio-teardown-writeup

## When to use
[OWNER] finished a build and wants public proof that shows how he thinks, not what the client
earned. Input: the build (niche, what it replaced, the decisions he made and why). Output: a
teardown write-up for the site portfolio, a LinkedIn post, or a proposal exhibit. This is the
CRAFT lane. For a results-and-outcomes proof piece use `case-study-writer`; for the close-day
win use `win-announcement`. This skill fires when the work is good but the numbers aren't in
yet, or when the point is judgment, not ROI.

## The core idea: sell the decision, not the outcome
Anyone can post a pretty screenshot. What sells [OWNER] is the reasoning: he moved the booking
button above the fold because the medspa loses the 9pm impulse booker without it. The
commentary IS the product. Each decision follows the same shape: what was wrong, what he did,
why it matters to the business. No results claim required, which is exactly why this works on
a fresh build with no data.

## What you need (facts only)
1. **The before.** What the old site did wrong, the 2-4 real faults. If there's a teardown on
   file, pull from it. Specific ("2 photos total, booking three clicks deep") beats "it was
   dated."
2. **The after.** The 3-5 decisions he made, each a deliberate choice with a reason. Not a
   feature list, a judgment list.
3. **The niche logic.** Why these choices fit THIS kind of business (medspa books from a phone
   at night; a plumber needs the phone number huge; a restaurant needs the menu and the
   reservation, nothing else).

## The write-up shape
- **Opener:** the receipt or the diagnosis flip. "This medspa had six photos and a booking
  button three clicks deep. Here's the rebuild." First line carries the problem.
- **The decisions, 3-5 of them.** Each: the fault, the fix, the one-line why-it-matters. This
  is the body and the whole value. One idea per decision, no triads.
- **The through-line:** one sentence naming the operator principle behind all of them ("Every
  choice served one job: get the phone booking before the visitor's attention breaks").
- **Close:** a hard stop for a pure-craft post, or the scope-filter offer if the job is to
  sell ("If your builds look like the before, send me the scope").
- **Length:** post 90-160 words; portfolio exhibit can run fuller; proposal version tightens
  to one paragraph.

## Anonymization and honesty (hard)
- **Anonymize by default.** "A medspa in the Southeast," "a plumbing company." Name the client
  only with a recorded OK. White-label end clients: never, NDA.
- **Craft claims only, no invented results.** He may say what he built and why. He may NOT say
  "bookings went up 40%" unless the client reported it and it's on file. The whole point of
  this lane is that it needs no outcome, so never borrow one.
- **The before must be true.** If he's describing the old site's faults, they were real. No
  strawman "before" to make the after look better.
- **Screens are optional and must be his.** Only his own build shots, never a competitor's or
  a stock mockup passed off as the work.

## Voice (hard rules)
No em-dashes or en-dashes, ever. Short sentences, 9-13 words. Contractions always. No emojis,
0-2 hashtags on LinkedIn, no engagement bait. The five AI-LinkedIn patterns from
`linkedin-post-writer` are banned here too: no "here's what I learned," no broetry line
breaks, no cliche hooks, no rule-of-three crutch, no emoji bullets. Full spec:
`~/Claude/business-library/VOICE-SPEC.md`.

## Output structure (produce this)
1. **The teardown post** (LinkedIn / portfolio), ready to paste.
2. **The one-paragraph proposal version** (the same teardown, compressed to a proof exhibit).
3. **A note** on what to anonymize and which screens (if any) are safe to attach.

## WORKED EXAMPLE — a medspa rebuild, pure craft
*Input: [OWNER] rebuilt a medspa site. Before: a Fresha rental page with 4 photos, no team
credentials, booking two taps deep, prices hidden. After decisions: booking widget embedded
above the fold, license badges next to each injector, a consented before/after gallery, a
"from $X" price menu, a GLP-1 membership page. No results data yet, live one week. Anonymize
(no naming OK).*

**The teardown post:**

> This medspa was selling $600 injectables off a Fresha rental page with four photos and no
> injector credentials. Here's the rebuild and why each call was made.
>
> Booking widget moved above the fold. This audience books from Instagram at 9pm, and every
> tap between them and the calendar is a lost patient.
>
> License badges next to each injector. Patients vet who's putting a needle in their face.
> Hiding the credentials hides the trust.
>
> Real prices, "from $X," on the menu. Hidden pricing sends people to the competitor who
> shows theirs. It doesn't protect margin, it leaks leads.
>
> A dedicated GLP-1 membership page, not a paragraph. That's the fastest-growing line in the
> practice. It earns its own room.
>
> Every decision served one job: get the booking before the visitor's attention breaks.
>
> That's the work.

**The one-paragraph proposal version:**

> Recent build: a medspa running $600 injectables off a Fresha rental page. Rebuilt with
> booking above the fold, injector licenses visible, a "from $X" price menu, and a dedicated
> membership page. Every choice aimed at one thing, capturing the late-night phone booking
> before attention breaks. Craft-first, built to the niche.

**Anonymization note:** keep it "a medspa," no city precise enough to identify, no client name
until a recorded OK lands (the +14 day testimonial ask is where that usually comes). Safe to
attach: [OWNER]'s own before/after screenshots of the build, with any patient photos in the
gallery blurred or omitted unless signed consent is on file. Never attach the client's logo.

*Why this works: no results claim anywhere, yet it sells hard, because the reasoning shows an
operator who understands the business. The through-line ties it together. It's usable the day
the build goes live, before a single booking is counted.*
