# recruiter-screen-call

## When to use
A recruiter or in-house sourcer booked a 15-30 minute intro call. This is not the
hiring-manager interview, it's the gate before it. Input: the posting and company.
Output: a half-page card [OWNER] reads once and walks in ready. For the deep behavioral
prep use `interview-ace`; for the money moment use `salary-negotiation`. This skill
owns the screen only.

## What the screen actually is (frame it right)
The recruiter is not evaluating depth. They're checking three boxes: can he do the job
on paper, will the comp range work, and is he normal to talk to. Most candidates lose
the screen by rambling. The whole game is short, warm, confident answers that leave the
recruiter nothing to worry about when they brief the hiring manager. Every answer under
30 seconds. Land the number, then stop.

## The 3 questions they always open with
1. **"Walk me through your background."** 30 seconds, not your life story. Shape: what
   he is now (runs [OWNER_COMPANY]), the one number (scaled an agency [PRIOR_RESULT]/yr
   as fractional COO), and why this role. End on their role, not on you.
2. **"What are you looking for in your next role?"** Point it straight at their posting:
   one company to go deep on, the specific mandate they wrote. Never a list of wants.
3. **"What are your salary expectations?"** The deflection line below. Never blurt a
   number to a recruiter cold; try to get their band first.

## The salary-deflection line (recruiter screen only)
"I'd want to understand the scope before we anchor on a number. What's the band budgeted
for this seat?" *(If pressed once:)* "Based on the scope and my track record I'm looking
around [SALARY_ANCHOR], flexible for the right equity and role." *(Then stop talking.)* Full comp
choreography lives in `salary-negotiation`; on the screen, deflect once, answer once,
move on. Floor is $110K, never anchor below it. Band tops [CARE_GROWTH]K+ means anchor [CARE_GROWTH]-175K.

## "Why are you leaving / why us" in 20 seconds each
- **Why leaving:** "I run my own shop and I've built the operator playbook across a lot
  of clients. I want to point that at one company's growth instead of spreading across
  ten." Never badmouth a client, a market, or anyone. Forward-looking, calm.
- **Why us:** one real, specific reason from THEIR business. Their stage, their mandate,
  something you actually read. "You're the first growth hire and the thesis is AI-leveraged
  headcount. That's already how I work." Generic enthusiasm reads as no reason at all.

## The record (say it once, let it carry)
Six years. Fractional COO who scaled a marketing agency [PRIOR_RESULT]/yr. One operator
running team-level output with AI. Never inflate it, the number does the work. If a tool
gap comes up (they run HubSpot, he's GHL): "I built an entire operating system on GHL, so
I know CRM and automation at the architecture level. The concepts transfer and I ramp
fast." Never fake tool-years.

## Voice (hard rules)
No em-dashes or en-dashes, ever. Short sentences, 9-13 words. Contractions always. Sounds
like a competent operator on the phone, not a candidate performing. No throat-clearing, no
over-explaining. Full spec: `~/Claude/business-library/VOICE-SPEC.md`.

## Output structure (produce exactly this, half a page)
1. **2-line company read:** what they do and stage · what this screen is really checking.
2. **The 3 openers**, each with his 30-second answer tailored to this role.
3. **Salary line** for this band (deflect, then the number if pressed).
4. **Why-leaving / why-us**, both filled with a real reason from this company.
5. **One question to ask the recruiter** (signals he's evaluating them back).
6. **The one landmine** on this specific screen.

## WORKED EXAMPLE — CacheFly, Growth Marketing screen (remote, US)
*Input: CacheFly, a CDN / edge-delivery company. Recruiter screen for a growth-marketing
seat. Posting leans on demand gen, funnel ownership, and reporting for a technical buyer.*

**Company read:** CDN / content-delivery infrastructure, buyers are engineering and
platform teams. This screen is checking three things: can he own demand gen end to end,
does the comp range work, and is he easy to hand to the VP.

**The 3 openers:**
- *Background (30 sec):* "I run a web and automation shop, [OWNER_COMPANY]. Before and
  around that I was fractional COO of a marketing agency and scaled it from [PRIOR_BASELINE] to over
  $1M a year, building the growth engine with systems instead of headcount. I'm looking to
  point that at one company's funnel now, which is why your growth seat caught me."
- *What you're looking for:* "One company to go deep on. Your posting is full funnel and
  reporting owned by one operator. That's the work I do best."
- *Salary:* "I'd want to understand the scope before we anchor. What's the band for this
  seat?" *(If pressed:)* "Around [SALARY_ANCHOR], flexible for the right equity and role."

**Salary line:** deflect first. If they name a band topping [CARE_GROWTH]K+, anchor [CARE_GROWTH]-175K.
Floor $110K, never spoken.

**Why leaving:** "I've run the operator playbook across a lot of clients. I want to build
one growth engine deep instead of spreading thin." **Why us:** "You sell to engineers, and
I've spent years turning technical delivery into revenue. Selling speed and reliability to
a technical buyer is a motion I know."

**Question to ask the recruiter:** "What's the one thing the hiring manager most wants this
person to fix in the first six months?" (Gets you the real mandate before the next round.)

**The landmine:** the technical-buyer gap. If the recruiter probes "you've sold to SMBs,
can you sell to engineers?", name it once and pivot: "The audience is new, the motion isn't.
Turning delivery into pipeline is exactly what I did at the agency." Do not oversell CDN
knowledge you don't have on a screen. Save depth for the hiring-manager round.
