# referral-partner-agreement

## When to use
[OWNER] wants to set up a repeatable referral channel: someone who regularly meets businesses
needing a site (a designer, a copywriter, a marketing freelancer, a booking-software rep) sends
those clients to [OWNER] for a cut. Input: the partner type and how they meet clients. Output:
the recruit pitch that signs them, and the plain-terms one-pager that defines the deal. This is
a standing channel, different from `referral-ask`, which is a one-time favor from a happy
client. This skill recruits partners who send business on purpose, repeatedly.

## The load-bearing money fact (read this first)
The Referral-Partner SKU is **[REFERRAL_FEE] per closed referral**, and it is **PROPOSED-v2** in
`offers.md`, not confirmed. **Do not promise the [REFERRAL_FEE] payout as a finalized, guaranteed term
in anything client-facing until [OWNER] confirms it.** Frame it as the intended structure he's
building, or gate it behind "here's how I'd structure it." Once [OWNER] confirms the number and
the payout mechanics, it graduates to a firm term. Until then: real intent, not a signed
promise. Everything else in the agreement (invisibility, how referrals are handled, the no-fit
courtesy) is safe to state plainly now.

## Who makes a good partner (steer the recruit)
- **Web designers who don't build / don't want to.** They design, [OWNER] builds, or they hand
  off overflow. Highest-fit.
- **Copywriters and marketing freelancers** whose clients keep asking "can you also do the
  site?" (Note: [OWNER]'s dad runs [a family member's site]; copy is the dad's lane, keep that clean.)
- **Booking-software or CRM reps** (Fresha, Vagaro, GHL resellers) whose clients need a site
  around the tool.
- **Agencies with a bottleneck** are the white-label offer, not this channel; route them to
  `agency-partner-pitch`. This SKU is for individuals and small vendors sending one client at
  a time.

## The recruit pitch (four beats)
1. **Their angle, not yours.** Open with what's in it for them: a clean handoff for work they
   can't or won't do, a client kept happy, and a cut. Lead with their upside.
2. **The invisibility promise.** Their client stays their client. [OWNER] builds under the
   partner's brand if they want (white-label) or as a named vendor if they prefer, their call.
   [OWNER] never poaches. This is the trust that makes a partner send a second client.
3. **The structure, honestly.** How a referral works: they intro, [OWNER] scopes it, gives the
   day-3 preview and approve-before-live treatment, and a kind no if it's not a fit. Then the
   payout, framed per the money-fact rule above ([REFERRAL_FEE]/closed, as the intended structure until
   confirmed).
4. **One easy first step.** "Send me one client you've got sitting in the 'needs a site' pile.
   Let's run it once and see how clean it is." Low commitment, one referral, proof by doing.

## The plain-terms one-pager (what the agreement states)
Short, plain English, explicitly **not legal advice** (bigger arrangements route to
`contract-sow-drafter`). It names:
- **What triggers a payout:** a referral that closes and pays a deposit. Not a lead, not a
  maybe. (Payout number per the money-fact rule.)
- **Invisibility terms:** white-label or named vendor, partner's choice; [OWNER] never markets to
  the referred client directly beyond the build; the client relationship stays the partner's.
- **How referrals are handled:** same build standard every client gets, day-3 preview,
  approve-before-live, a kind no on non-fits so the partner's name is never spent on a bad
  match.
- **No exclusivity, no quota.** The partner sends when they've got someone. No pressure, no
  minimums.
- **How the partner gets paid / tracked:** stated simply once the mechanics are confirmed;
  until then, "we'll settle per closed referral, structure confirmed before your first one."

## Hard lines
- **Never state [REFERRAL_FEE] as a locked, guaranteed payout** in a partner-facing doc until [OWNER]
  confirms it. Intended structure, yes; signed promise, no.
- **Protect the partner's client relationship in writing.** The whole channel dies if a partner
  fears being cut out. Say the invisibility rule explicitly.
- **This is not legal advice.** The one-pager is a working understanding. Anything formal goes
  through `contract-sow-drafter` and, for real contracts, a lawyer.
- **Keep copy in the dad's lane.** Don't recruit partners in a way that competes with
  [a family member's site].

## Voice (hard rules)
No em-dashes or en-dashes, ever. Short sentences, 9-13 words. Contractions always. No emojis.
The pitch reads like one operator offering another a clean deal, not a network-marketing
recruit. No hype, no "passive income" language. Sign "[OWNER]." Full spec:
`~/Claude/business-library/VOICE-SPEC.md`.

## Output structure (produce this)
1. **The recruit pitch** (email or DM), ready to send to a specific partner.
2. **The plain-terms one-pager**, the working agreement in plain English.
3. **A flag** noting the [REFERRAL_FEE] payout is PROPOSED until [OWNER] confirms, and where it's stated
   as intent vs promise.

## WORKED EXAMPLE — pitching a web designer as a referral partner
*Input: Maya, a freelance web designer who does brand and UX but hands off or turns down
full-build work, especially anything with booking or e-commerce. [OWNER] met her in a Slack
community. Goal: recruit her as a standing referral partner.*

**The recruit pitch (DM/email):**

> Maya, quick one. You mentioned you pass on the heavier build jobs, the booking and e-com
> stuff. I build exactly those, fast, and I want to make your overflow easy money instead of a
> "sorry, not my thing."
>
> Here's the deal. When a client needs a full build you don't want, you intro us. I build it
> under your brand if you want to stay the face of it, or as your named developer if you'd
> rather. Your client stays your client. I never market to them past the build.
>
> They get a working preview on day 3 and approve everything before it goes live, same as all
> my clients, and if it's not a fit I tell them kindly so your name's never on a bad match.
>
> I'm setting up a per-closed-referral cut for partners like you. I'll confirm the exact number
> before your first one so it's clean. Want to run one client through and see how it goes? Send
> me someone sitting in your "needs a real site" pile.
>
> [OWNER]

**The plain-terms one-pager (the working understanding):**

> **Referral partnership, plain terms (a working understanding, not a legal contract):**
>
> - You send me clients who need a website build you don't want to take.
> - I build it white-label under your brand, or as your named developer. Your call, per client.
> - Your client stays yours. I don't market to them beyond the build itself.
> - Every referral gets the same standard: a working preview on day 3, approval before
>   anything goes live, and an honest no if it's not a fit.
> - A referral counts once it closes and pays a deposit. Not a lead, a signed build.
> - Per closed referral I pay you a partner cut. I'll confirm the exact figure with you before
>   your first referral so there's no ambiguity.
> - No exclusivity, no quota. Send business when you've got it.
>
> Anything bigger than this gets a real agreement in writing.

**Flag:** the payout is the [REFERRAL_FEE]-per-closed-referral SKU, which is still PROPOSED-v2 in
`offers.md`. Both the pitch and the one-pager deliberately say "I'll confirm the exact number
before your first one," never "[REFERRAL_FEE] guaranteed." Once [OWNER] confirms the figure and how it's
paid, swap in "[REFERRAL_FEE] per closed referral, paid within [X] days of the deposit" and it becomes a
firm term.
