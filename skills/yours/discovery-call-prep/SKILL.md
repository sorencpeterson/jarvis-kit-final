# discovery-call-prep

## When to use
Any booked call with a prospect: warm-list contact, GHL lead, referral, cold replier who
asked to talk. Output is a one-page call sheet [OWNER] can glance at mid-call. It is prep,
not a script. If the prospect's site or context is missing, pull it before writing;
a call sheet with no real detail is a horoscope.

## Inputs
1. **Who**: name, business, niche, where they came from (GHL tag, warm list, referral).
2. **Their web presence**: the actual site or booking page. Skim it like a customer
   would. Real observed faults feed the read. Never invent faults.
3. **The prior touch**: what they said in the reply, the form, or the last call.

## Pricing steers (the only tiers you may aim at)
| Situation | Steer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Local service with no booking motion (plumber, roofer, landscaper) | Standard [STANDARD_SITE] | The default |
| Takes appointments (medspa, salon, gym, dental, restaurant), even if they only book by phone today | E-com/Booking [ECOM_PRICE] | Booking IS the business |
| Medspa with a dead site or Fresha/Vagaro-only page | White-Glove [WHITE_GLOVE] | They need copy + brand + structure, not a reskin |
| Agency, white-label | First build [FIRST_BUILD] flat, then rate card | The proving rate |
| Salvageable existing site | Webfix [WEBFIX] | Unless 4+ structural faults, then rebuild |
| No site at all (non-medspa) | Landing [LANDING_PAGE], Standard as follow-on | Start fast |

Hard terms that ride along: 50% deposit books the slot. Rush +50%. Two revision rounds
included. Price holds 14 days. Max discount 15% and only traded. Never hourly.
Full tree: `business-library/playbooks/pricing-tree.md`.

## The call sheet (5 parts, always this order)
1. **The read.** Three lines on their actual situation. What they sell, what their web
   presence does to it, what that costs them. Written so [OWNER] could say it out loud.
2. **The five questions, in order.** Money questions come first or second, always:
   "what's a job/patient/client worth to you?" gets THEIR number on the table before any
   price of ours does. Their number does the selling. Each question earns a note on what
   it sets up. Medspa wedge questions live in
   `business-library/sops/niche-books/medspa.md`.
3. **The tier steer.** One tier, one price, one line of why, from the table above. Name
   the ROI anchor in their units (one missed job, two retained patients, their markup).
4. **The close plan.** Define what a yes looks like on THIS call. Hot lead: deposit link
   on the line plus first intake item ("tap the link, send me your logo"). Not there yet:
   a dated, named next step before hanging up. "I'll send info" is not a close plan.
5. **The landmine.** The one thing that kills this specific deal, stated so it can't be
   stepped on. Usually a wrong assumption (replacing their booking system, threatening an
   agency's client relationship, jargon at a clinical owner).

## Voice (hard rules)
The sheet is for [OWNER]'s eyes but every quoted line must survive being said out loud.
No em-dashes or en-dashes, ever. Short sentences, 9-13 words. Contractions always. No
emojis. Numbers do the talking. Banned: unlock, leverage, seamless, elevate, excited,
delve, circle back. Say the counter or the price, then stop talking. Full spec:
`business-library/VOICE-SPEC.md`.

## True facts you may state
Six years doing this, never rounded up. Fractional COO who scaled a marketing agency
[PRIOR_RESULT] per year. 35+ white-label builds, 48-72 hr first draft for agencies.
Day-3 working preview, live 7 days from deposit. NDA standard for agencies. A retained
medspa tox patient is worth about [PROJECT_EXAMPLE] a year. Never claim named clients, review
counts, or guaranteed results.

## WORKED EXAMPLE 1 — medspa, Fresha-only presence
*Input: Kelsey, owner-injector (RN), Glow Theory Aesthetics, Simi Valley. Warm list.
Her Instagram bio link goes straight to a Fresha booking page. No site of her own.
Runs tox, filler, facials. Replied asking about "getting a real website."*

**CALL SHEET | Kelsey, Glow Theory Aesthetics**

**The read.** Her entire web presence is a Fresha rental page: no faces, no prices, no
before/afters, no proof. She sells trust and results and shows neither. She's paying
for Instagram attention and dropping the catch at the last step.

**The five questions, in order.**
1. "What's a new patient worth to you in the first year, honestly?" (Money first. Her
   number sells the site. If she doesn't know: a retained tox patient runs about [PROJECT_EXAMPLE]
   a year. Say it, then stop.)
2. "Where do bookings come from now, and what happens to someone who finds you at 9pm?"
   (Sets up booking above the fold. Fresha stays, embedded.)
3. "Are you running a weight-loss program yet? Where does someone sign up for it?"
   (The GLP-1 line is the growth revenue. It gets its own page, not a paragraph.)
4. "What did your last marketing spend produce that you could actually count?"
   (Positions the fixed-price build against the retainer she's probably been burned by.)
5. "If the calendar filled tomorrow, which services do you want filling it?"
   (Steers the build toward her high-margin lines, and tells you what the hero sells.)

**The steer: White-Glove, [WHITE_GLOVE].** She has no site, so this isn't a reskin. Copy,
brand, photo direction, and the build, with her Fresha booking embedded above the fold.
The anchor, said in her units: "Two retained tox patients pay for the whole site."
Care Growth+ at [CARE_PREMIUM] a month is the attach, but at delivery, not on this call.

**The close plan.** A yes today is the 50% deposit, [DEPOSIT_EXAMPLE], plus the intake list
started. The line: "Tap the deposit link and send me your logo. You see a working
preview on day 3." If a practice manager signs off too, book the 10-minute three-way
call before hanging up. Nothing leaves this call without a date on it.

**The landmine.** Do not propose replacing Fresha. Her booking history, memberships,
and cards on file live there. Suggest a migration and the deal dies on the spot. The
site catches, Fresha books. Second wire: she's clinical-first. Talk patient volume and
no-shows, never CTR or funnels.

## WORKED EXAMPLE 2 — agency owner, white-label overflow
*Input: Marcus, founder of Halyard Creative, 6-person brand agency. Replied to the cold
sequence asking how white-label overflow works. Portfolio is brand identity work, no
dev capability anywhere on their site.*

**CALL SHEET | Marcus, Halyard Creative**

**The read.** Brand shop with no build muscle. Every identity project that needs a site
is either painful or passed. He asked how overflow works, which means the volume
already exists. He isn't shopping features, he's deciding whether I'm safe to route
real client work through.

**The five questions, in order.**
1. "When a client needs the site to go with the rebrand, what do you quote, and who
   builds it now?" (Money first. His markup is the pitch: he quotes $3,000, my invoice
   is [STANDARD_SITE], the rest is margin he's turning away.)
2. "How many web projects did you pass on or lose in the last quarter?" (Prices the
   leak in his own numbers.)
3. "What broke with the last dev you used?" (Opens the reliability wound. Answer with
   the receipt: handed off Monday, client-ready Thursday, unlimited revisions until his
   client approves.)
4. "If fulfillment were solved, how many builds a month would you route?" (Sizes the
   relationship. 3+ a month earns 10% partner pricing later. Don't lead with that.)
5. "How does client approval run on your side, and who's my one point of contact?"
   (Invisibility mechanics. He owns the relationship, I never touch his client.)

**The steer: first build [FIRST_BUILD] flat.** The proving rate, first order only. Then the
rate card: [STANDARD_SITE] standard, [LANDING_PAGE] landing, [ECOM_PRICE] e-com or booking. 48-72 hour first
draft, unlimited revisions until his client signs off, NDA standard.

**The close plan.** A yes today is the scope of one real project in my inbox before
dinner. The line: "Send me the scope on whichever project is most stuck. I'll tell you
if I can help, and the first one runs at [FIRST_BUILD] flat." Deposit of $500 books the slot.

**The landmine.** Never sound like a threat to his client relationships. No "I could
also help your clients directly," not even as a joke. He's buying invisibility. Say
the NDA and his-brand-on-everything out loud, unprompted, before he has to ask.
