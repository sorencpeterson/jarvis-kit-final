# multi-offer-negotiation

## When to use
[OWNER] has (or is about to have) more than one live offer, or one offer plus a second process
far enough along to matter. Input: the offers or the stages, the numbers, and which company
he actually prefers. Output: the timeline choreography, the leverage email to the preferred
company, and the walk-away math. For a single offer's counter, use `salary-negotiation`;
this skill is for the two-body problem.

## The one rule that governs everything
**Never bluff an offer that doesn't exist.** A fabricated competing offer is the one move
that can blow up the whole thing, and the recruiting world is smaller than it looks. Every
number he cites must be real and defensible if they ask to see it. Real leverage, calmly
carried, wins. Fake leverage, once caught, loses the offer and the reputation.

## [OWNER]'s numbers (the anchors, never improvise)
Default ask [SALARY_ANCHOR], flexible for the right equity and role. Floor $110K. Band tops [CARE_GROWTH]K+
means anchor [CARE_GROWTH]-175K. The competing offer doesn't change his floor, it changes his
confidence. His real BATNA is [OWNER_COMPANY], which bills real money, so he can always
walk. That calm is the leverage, not the second offer.

## Step 1 — align the timelines (buy the runway)
The whole game is getting both offers live in the same window so a real choice exists. Two
moves:
- **Slow the one you want less.** "I'm excited and I want to give you a real answer. Can I
  come back to you by [date]?" Recruiters expect a few days; a respectful ask for time never
  loses an offer.
- **Speed the one you want more.** Tell the preferred company, honestly, that another offer
  is landing and you'd rather choose them. This is the single strongest accelerant in
  hiring: nothing moves a hiring committee faster than a good candidate about to be taken.

If they can't be aligned and the lesser offer explodes first, fall back to the walk-away
math below and decide on the numbers, not on fear.

## Step 2 — the honest-leverage email (to the preferred company)
Four beats, in order: (1) they're his first choice, stated plainly, (2) the true fact of the
other offer with its real number, (3) one clear condition that closes it, (4) a date. No
apology, no hedging, no "I hate to do this." It's not a threat, it's a candidate being
transparent about a real situation. Template in the worked example.

## Step 3 — the walk-away math (decide on numbers, not nerves)
When both are live, compare on the real total, not the base sticker:
- **Total comp year one:** base + realistic sign-on + equity he'd actually value (discount
  early-stage equity hard) + benefits delta.
- **The work:** which mandate does he actually want, which one grows him. A worse-paid role
  he wants can beat a better-paid one he doesn't, but name the gap in dollars so it's a
  choice, not a rationalization.
- **The BATNA line:** what does another quarter of [OWNER_COMPANY] clear? If neither offer
  beats staying independent on both money and appeal, walking is a real answer, not a
  failure.
- **The floor check:** if the preferred offer can't clear $110K even after the counter,
  it's not the preferred offer anymore. The number is a hard gate.

## Hard lines
- One condition per email, one date. Never a moving target of demands.
- Enthusiasm before every number, so nobody fears a rescind.
- Never play the same company twice with escalating "actually, one more thing." Counter
  once, cleanly.
- If the lesser company is used purely as a clock, still treat their people with respect.
  The industry remembers.

## Voice (hard rules)
No em-dashes or en-dashes, ever. Short sentences, 9-13 words. Contractions always. Warm,
transparent, immovable on the number. No apology anywhere near the money. Sign "[OWNER]."
Full spec: `business-library/VOICE-SPEC.md`.

## Output structure (produce this)
1. **Timeline plan:** what to say to each company to get both live in one window.
2. **The leverage email** to the preferred company, ready to send.
3. **The walk-away math:** the total-comp comparison and the decision line.
4. **One flag:** the risk in this specific pairing and how to hold it.

## WORKED EXAMPLE — two offers, one preferred
*Input: Offer A, from the company [OWNER] wants, Growth Marketing Manager, $125K base, small
early-stage equity, wants an answer by Friday. Offer B, a company he'd take but doesn't
love, $145K base, no equity, offer arriving Monday. He wants A, at a better number.*

**Timeline plan:** A is his choice but priced low and on the clock. B is higher and lands
Monday. Move: tell A the truth to accelerate their best number, and don't let B rush him.
- *To A (call or email):* "You're my first choice, and I want to be straight with you: I
  have another offer landing Monday at a higher base. I'd rather sign with you. Can we talk
  numbers before Friday?" That gives A a reason and a deadline to sharpen.
- *To B (once it lands):* "Thank you, I'm taking this seriously. Can I come back to you by
  Wednesday?" Buys the window without a lie.

**The leverage email to A:**

> Subject: Re: Offer — you're my first choice, one number to solve
>
> Hi Priya,
>
> I want to be direct because I respect your time. You're my first choice on the work, and
> I'm ready to sign this week.
>
> Being transparent: I have a second offer landing Monday at $145K base with no equity.
> Yours is the role I actually want. As fractional COO I scaled an agency from [PRIOR_BASELINE] to over
> $1M a year, and I run the full growth stack solo, so I'm confident I earn the top of your
> band.
>
> If you can get base to [SALARY_TARGET], I'll sign yours and close the other out this week. If base
> is capped, I'm open to bridging it with a sign-on or equity we put in writing.
>
> Either way, glad to be here.
>
> [OWNER]

**The walk-away math:**
- *Offer A adjusted:* [SALARY_TARGET] base (the ask) + early-stage equity discounted to near-zero for
  planning = call it [SALARY_TARGET] real, plus a role he wants.
- *Offer B:* $145K base, no equity, a role he'd tolerate. Real total roughly $145K.
- *The line:* if A gets to [SALARY_TARGET], the $5K gap buys a role he actually wants and equity
  upside. Take A. If A won't move past $130K, the gap to B widens to $15K for a job he likes
  less, and now it's a genuine coin-flip he decides on preference. Both clear the $110K floor,
  so the floor isn't the deciding factor here, the work and the delta are.
- *BATNA check:* both beat a slow quarter of [OWNER_COMPANY] on cash, so walking isn't the
  play. It stays in his pocket as the reason he can hold the number without flinching.

**One flag:** don't let A's Friday deadline stampede him into signing under $130K out of fear
B falls through. B is real and arriving Monday. He negotiates A from calm, not from a
countdown, because he genuinely has somewhere to stand.
