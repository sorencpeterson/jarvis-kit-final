# scope-creep-response

## When to use
Any "while you're in there, can you also..." from an active or delivered client:
another page, a new feature, a third round of changes, a drip of one-off edits.
Input is the ask verbatim, the client's tier and deposit status, and how many
revision rounds they've used. Output is the reply, ready to send. Spine is
objection bank #38: "That's outside this scope, and I protect your price by
protecting the scope."

## First, route it honestly (not everything that feels like creep is creep)
| The ask | The route | The price |
|---|---|---|
| Changes to existing pages, rounds 1-2 | Included round. Say yes warmly. | $0, one consolidated list |
| Changes to existing pages, round 3+ | Extra round, was in the proposal | [CARE_GROWTH] per round |
| New page with its own goal (program, service, offer) | Landing build as an add-on | [LANDING_PAGE] |
| Booking, cart, or checkout added to a non-booking build | Tier conversation | [ECOM_PRICE] tier |
| Copy + brand direction added mid-build | Tier conversation | White-Glove [WHITE_GLOVE] |
| Pile of small fixes on a LIVE delivered site | Webfix bundle | [WEBFIX] |
| "Can you just keep updating it monthly" | Care plan | [CARE_BASIC] or [CARE_GROWTH]/mo ([CARE_PREMIUM] medspa) |

Writing content is never bundled into a build price: if the ask includes posts,
articles, or email copy, the page shell can be the [LANDING_PAGE] landing add-on but the
writing itself has no ladder price and gets "I'll quote that separately."

A **round** is one consolidated list of changes, submitted once. Drip-fed one-offs get
batched into a round, kindly: "save these up, send one list, I'll turn it in 48 hours."
White-label FIRST builds carry the trial guarantee instead: unlimited revisions until
the agency's client approves (offers.md). Ongoing white-label runs the standard
two-round rule from the rate card pitch.

## The message (3 beats, always this order)
1. **Acknowledge on merit.** One sentence, never two. If the idea is good for their
   business, say so. No fake enthusiasm, no "great question!"
2. **Name it as new scope, plainly.** "That's a new page, it's outside the scope we
   approved." Said once, one sentence. No hedging, no "unfortunately," no policy-speak.
3. **Price it and give the path.** The number from the table, when it starts (default:
   after this build delivers, the approved date is protected), and the deposit rule if
   it's a build ($400 books an [LANDING_PAGE] page). Then stop. No justifying the price.

## Hard lines
- **Zero apology.** The boundary protects their price and their date. Say that.
- **The date is sacred.** Mid-build additions queue behind delivery by default. Never
  silently absorb work that moves the timeline.
- **Never invent a price.** Every number comes from the ladder above. An ask with no
  ladder fit gets "I'll quote that separately" and [OWNER] sets the number.
- **Free once is the rate forever.** Don't comp new scope to be nice.

## Voice (hard rules)
No em-dashes or en-dashes, ever. Short sentences, 9-13 words. Contractions always.
No emojis. SMS 2 sentences, email 6 sentences max: count them before returning and
cut rather than run long. Never defensive, never groveling. Say the
price, then stop talking. Full spec: `business-library/VOICE-SPEC.md`.

## WORKED EXAMPLE 1: medspa asks for "just one more page" post-approval
*Input: Kelsey, Glow Theory Aesthetics, White-Glove [WHITE_GLOVE] build. Round 2 done, site
approved, goes live Thursday. She emails: "This looks amazing!! One thing, can we add
a quick page for our new weight-loss program before launch? Should be easy since
everything's built."*

**The reply (email):**

> Glad it landed. And a weight-loss program page is the right instinct, that's your
> highest-growth revenue line and it deserves its own page, not a paragraph.
>
> It's a new page with its own signup goal, which is outside the scope we approved,
> so here's how I'd run it: the site goes live Thursday as planned, and I build the
> program page as its own landing build right behind it, [LANDING_PAGE], live within a week of
> your go-ahead. That protects Thursday and gives the program a page built to convert
> instead of one rushed in before launch.
>
> Want it? $400 books it and I'll start Friday.

Why it works: the acknowledge is real (the niche book says GLP-1 pages ARE the growth
line). "New page with its own signup goal" names the scope line without policy-speak.
The date stays protected, the price is the ladder price, and the close is a deposit
ask, not a discussion. No apology anywhere.

## WORKED EXAMPLE 2: agency client "adding a second revision round" (false alarm)
*Input: ongoing white-label partner (not their first build), Standard [STANDARD_SITE] build for
their client. Round 1 delivered Monday. Their PM emails Wednesday: "Client came back
with another batch of changes, sorry. Can we squeeze these in? List attached, and
they mentioned they might have a few more next week."*

**The reply (email):**

> No apology needed, that's round 2 of 2 and it's included. Send the attached list
> over and I'll have it turned in 48 hours.
>
> One thing to flag now so nothing surprises anyone later: "a few more next week"
> would be round 3, and round 3 runs [CARE_GROWTH]. Worth telling your client this pass is
> the final polish, so everything they want lands on this list.

Why it works: the skill's first job is honest routing, and an included round is not
creep. Paying it gladly builds the partner relationship. The real boundary is the
drip: naming round 3 and its price NOW, while it's hypothetical, means the [CARE_GROWTH] is
never an argument later. The PM also gets a line they can forward to their client,
which is what a white-label partner actually needs.
