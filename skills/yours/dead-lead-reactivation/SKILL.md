# dead-lead-reactivation

## When to use
Working the 423 warm repliers (421 have an email) who replied to a [SECOND_BRAND]
campaign and never closed. Input: the contact, their niche, and whichever channel is
live (mostly email; SMS only if they've texted before). Two jobs this skill does: (1)
write the vague reactivation first-touch, and (2) triage whatever they reply and bridge
a hand-raise to a booked call. They came in about growing their business, NOT a website.
Re-open vague. This batch is the volume play behind the 58 booked calls, and it can load
into a paused GHL drip that sends itself. Full context: MONEY-THIS-MONTH.md Section 3.

## The reactivation angle (vague, by niche)
Same discipline as the call: no pitch, just re-open the door and ask if the need is
still live. Swap the noun to the niche.
| Niche | The re-open angle |
|---|---|
| Medspa / clinic / men's health | "still looking to bring in more patients, or has that been handled?" |
| HVAC / plumbing / contractor | "still looking to book more jobs, or did you get it handled?" |
| Salon / local service | "still looking to bring in more customers, or has that been handled?" |
| Mixed list / unknown | "still looking to bring in more customers, or has that been handled?" |

The "or has that been handled" gives the clean out that makes the yes real. The first
touch offers NOTHING. It asks one question and gives them an easy exit.

## Reply triage (three buckets, route on sight)
When they reply, sort it before you write back.
| Bucket | What it sounds like | The move |
|---|---|---|
| **Hand-raise** | "yes," "what do you have," "we could use that," a question about pricing or what you do | Bridge to a booked call. Do NOT pitch a price in the thread. Get them on the phone where the need-find and pivot happen. |
| **Polite-no** | "we're good for now," "maybe later," "not a priority" | One soft check-back, dated. "No worries. Want me to check back in the fall when {season} picks up?" Then leave them. |
| **Dead / remove** | "stop," "not interested," "remove me," anything hostile | Instant clean compliance (objection bank #47). "Done, you're off my list. If anything changes my name finds easy." Tag NO_GO and move on. |

## The re-engage to booked-call bridge (for a hand-raise)
The reply is warm but the call is where the money is. The bridge is short, references
nothing they have to remember, and drives to one link.
- **Acknowledge the raise, don't pitch it.** "Perfect. Rather than trade emails, 15
  minutes on the phone and I can tell you exactly what I'd do."
- **Give a reason it's a call, not a quote.** "It depends on where your leads are
  leaking, and I can't see that over email." Honest, and it moves them to the phone.
- **One ask, one link.** "Grab a time here: [OWNER_SITE]/book" Then stop.
- On the call, run the warm-reopen-call-script: need-find, then pivot to the offer that
  fits. The reactivation email's only job is to buy the call.

## Voice (hard rules)
No em-dashes or en-dashes, ever, subject lines included. Email under 90 words, one link,
sign "[OWNER]" alone. SMS 1-3 sentences, only if they've texted before, never cold SMS.
Short sentences, 9-13 words. Contractions always. No emojis. First line carries it, zero
throat-clearing. End on the ask, then stop. Banned: unlock, leverage, seamless, elevate,
excited, circle back. Full spec: `~/Claude/business-library/VOICE-SPEC.md`.

## True facts you may state
Six years doing this. Fractional COO who scaled a marketing agency [PRIOR_RESULT] per
year. A retained medspa tox patient is worth about [PROJECT_EXAMPLE] a year. Never claim named
clients, review counts, or guaranteed results. Never reference what the original reply
said if you don't have it, re-open vague and assume they've forgotten the thread.

## WORKED EXAMPLE 1 — medspa replier, first touch then a hand-raise
*Input: Priya, medspa owner. Replied to a [SECOND_BRAND] email months ago, went cold.
Email on file. Medspa, so the patient angle.*

**1. The reactivation email (vague, no pitch):**
> Subject: still on your radar?
>
> Hey Priya,
>
> [OWNER] with [SECOND_BRAND]. You reached out a while back and we never took it all the
> way, so I wanted to check back.
>
> Quick question. Are you still looking to bring in more patients, or has that been
> handled since we talked?
>
> If it's still on your list, I've got a couple of ideas for practices like yours and
> it's an easy 15-minute conversation. If not, no worries, just reply and I'll take you
> off my follow-up.
>
> Grab a time here: [OWNER_SITE]/book
>
> [OWNER]

**2. She replies: "Yeah honestly bookings have been slow, what do you have in mind?"**

That's a hand-raise. Do NOT quote a price. Bridge to the call:
> Perfect. Rather than trade emails, give me 15 minutes on the phone and I'll tell you
> exactly what I'd do. It depends on where your bookings are leaking and I can't see
> that from here. Grab a time: [OWNER_SITE]/book

Why: the first touch offers nothing and hands her a clean out, which is what makes
"bookings have been slow" a real raise. The bridge reads the raise, refuses to pitch a
number in email, and gives one honest reason it's a call. The pivot to White-Glove or
Booking happens live, after the need-find, never in the thread.

## WORKED EXAMPLE 2 — local-service replier, a polite-no and a dead one
*Input: Carl, owner of a landscaping company. Replied months ago, went cold. Email on
file. Two branches shown: the polite-no and the flat remove.*

**1. The reactivation email (contractor angle):**
> Subject: still on your radar?
>
> Hey Carl,
>
> [OWNER] with [SECOND_BRAND]. You reached out a while back and we never took it
> anywhere, so I wanted to check back.
>
> Quick one. Are you still looking to book more jobs, or did you get that handled?
>
> If it's still on your list, I've got a couple of ideas for outfits like yours and it's
> a quick 15-minute call. If not, no worries, just reply and I'll take you off my
> follow-up.
>
> Grab a time here: [OWNER_SITE]/book
>
> [OWNER]

**2a. He replies: "We're pretty booked through summer, maybe later."**

Polite-no. One dated check-back, then leave him:
> No worries at all, Carl. Want me to check back in early fall when things slow down and
> you're filling the winter calendar? Either way, my name finds easy if anything changes.

**2b. He replies: "Not interested, take me off this."**

Dead. Instant clean compliance, no sell:
> Done, you're off my list. If anything changes down the road, my name finds easy. Take
> care.

Then tag NO_GO and move on.

Why: the same vague first touch works for a contractor, just swap "patients" for "jobs."
The polite-no gets one soft, dated check-back tied to his real season (fall, when
landscaping slows), never a re-sell. The remove gets instant clean compliance with zero
friction, which protects the sender reputation and is the rule (bank #47). No guilt, no
pitch, no second ask.
