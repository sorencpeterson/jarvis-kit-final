# case-study-writer

## When to use
A build is delivered (or a deal is won) and [OWNER] wants proof assets out of it. Input
is the record: before-state, what shipped, real numbers, dates. Output is three
artifacts, each ready to use. Nothing publishes without [OWNER]'s click.

## Inputs
1. **The before-state**: the teardown, what was broken, why they came. If it's not on
   record, it does not exist for this case study.
2. **What shipped**: tier, scope, timeline, lane (white-label or direct).
3. **Real numbers**: price, days to close or deliver, anything measured after launch.
4. **What's a GAP**: the win records mark missing data explicitly. Respect the marks.

## The hard rule (this is the whole skill)
**Only verified facts from the input.** Every claim must trace to the record.
- Missing metrics get framed as **what was shipped and how fast**, never as outcomes.
  "Live in 7 days" is always available. "Bookings up 40%" needs a source or it dies.
- No fabricated quotes. A proposed pull quote stays labeled pending until the client
  signs off on it. Never publish it as something they said.
- **White-label clients stay anonymous in public artifacts** unless they approved being
  named. The NDA cuts both ways: [OWNER] stays invisible to their client, and the agency
  doesn't get outed as outsourcing. Describe by category ("a software agency").
- If the record is too thin for an artifact, say which fact is missing and stop. A thin
  true case study beats a rich invented one every time.

## The three artifacts
1. **Proof line** (1 sentence, for proposals). A number plus a timeline, receipt-shaped.
   "Handed off Monday, client-ready Thursday" is the model.
2. **LinkedIn post** (his voice). Opens with the receipt or the diagnosis flip, never
   "excited to share." Short sentences. One lesson, stated plainly. Ends on the flat
   offer statement or a short imperative. No hashtag piles, no cliches.
3. **Anonymized teardown paragraph** (for cold outreach). Niche, what happened, the
   number, the timeline. No names, nothing traceable. It must make a cold reader think
   "that could be me."

## Voice (hard rules)
No em-dashes or en-dashes, ever. Sentences 9-13 words, a 3-word sentence is a feature.
Contractions always. No emojis. Numbers do the talking. Banned: unlock, leverage,
seamless, elevate, excited, thrilled, game-changing, journey. Days of the week make
timelines real. Full spec: `~/Claude/business-library/VOICE-SPEC.md`.

## True facts you may state (background, beyond the input)
Six years doing this. Fractional COO who scaled an agency [PRIOR_RESULT] per year. 35+
white-label builds, 48-72 hr first draft. First agency build [FIRST_BUILD] flat, standard
[STANDARD_SITE]. NDA standard. Portfolio [OWNER_SITE]/web. Never claim named clients or
results that aren't in the record.

## WORKED EXAMPLE — the Acme Co Soft win
*Input, from the verified ledger record
(`~/Claude/second-brain/store/drafts/win_onepager.md`): software agency, came in cold
off the white-label webdev email list. Their info@ inbox replied. [STANDARD_SITE] white-label
build, logged as won 2026-07-04, roughly 3 days from deal opened to cash confirmed.
The proposal link was never opened; it closed in the conversation. GAPs on record: no
teardown, no scope detail, no delivery dates, no testimonial. A pull quote exists but
is pending client sign-off, so it is unusable.*

**Artifact 1 — proof line (for proposals):**
> The last agency deal went from cold reply to a closed [STANDARD_SITE] white-label build in 3
> days, and they never even opened the proposal link.

**Artifact 2 — LinkedIn post:**

A [STANDARD_SITE] build closed last week and the proposal was never opened.

Cold email to a software agency. They replied, we talked scope right there in the
thread, and three days later the deal was done. The proposal link is still sitting
there, unclicked.

The lesson isn't that proposals are dead. The lesson is that the proposal doesn't do
the selling. The conversation does. The paper just writes down a decision that already
got made.

So I stopped polishing documents and started answering replies faster.

White-label site builds for agencies. First one's [FIRST_BUILD] flat to prove it. Send me the
scope, I'll tell you if I can help.

**Artifact 3 — anonymized teardown paragraph (for cold outreach):**
> A software agency came off this same email list last week. They replied from their
> info@ inbox, we sorted scope in the thread, and three days later their first [STANDARD_SITE]
> white-label build was closed. They never opened the proposal. If you've got web work
> you're passing on, that's the whole model: your brand, my build, first draft in
> 48-72 hours.

**What stayed out, and why:** no before-state claims (no teardown on record), no
deliverable details (not on record), no "the client loved it" (no testimonial yet),
and the pull quote (pending sign-off, so it doesn't exist). The agency is named in
[OWNER]'s internal ledger but anonymized here: white-label partners don't get outed.
When the testimonial ask lands, rerun this skill and the artifacts get stronger.
