# niche-positioning-writer

## When to use
[OWNER] is opening a new vertical (a niche book doesn't exist yet, or exists thin) and needs
the positioning before the outreach, proposals, and posts get written. Input: the niche, and
whatever's known about how they make money and what their sites do wrong. Output: a wedge
plus a messaging spine other skills pull from (`money-outreach`, `money-proposal`,
`linkedin-post-writer`, `discovery-call-prep`). The existing beachhead is medspa
(`sops/niche-books/medspa.md`); this skill builds the next one to that standard.

## The wedge (the whole thing in one sentence)
A wedge is the single sentence that says why THIS niche needs [OWNER] specifically, right now.
It has three parts: what they sell, the gap between what they sell and what their site shows,
and why that gap costs them money they understand. Medspa's wedge: "They sell before-and-
afters and trust, and their sites bury the offer behind a Fresha rental page." Find the
equivalent one-liner for the new niche. If the wedge isn't sharp, nothing downstream lands.

## The four inputs to nail (research or ask, never invent)
1. **How they make money.** Ticket size, recurring vs one-off, what a retained customer is
   worth per year. Owners who understand customer-acquisition math buy fixed-price builds
   without flinching. Get the real economics.
2. **Who signs the check.** The persona, their bias, what they're allergic to. (Medspa:
   owner-injector, clinical-first, allergic to marketing jargon, talks patient volume not
   CTR.) Talk in their units.
3. **What their site does wrong.** The 2-4 structural faults common to the niche, ideally
   from a real teardown. This is the proof the wedge is true, not just clever.
4. **The urgency line.** The reason to act now: a seasonal deadline, a fast-growing revenue
   line, a competitor motion. (Medspa: the GLP-1 goldrush.) No urgency means slow deals.

## The messaging spine (what downstream skills reuse)
Produce these building blocks, each in his voice, so every later asset stays consistent:
- **The wedge sentence** (above), stated flat.
- **The diagnosis flip** for outreach and posts: "You don't have an X problem. You have a Y
  problem." Built from the wedge.
- **The tier steer:** which pricing-tree SKU fits the niche default, from
  `playbooks/pricing-tree.md`. Never invent a price; route to the ladder (Landing [LANDING_PAGE],
  Standard [STANDARD_SITE], E-com/Booking [ECOM_PRICE], White-Glove [WHITE_GLOVE]).
- **Three proof angles:** the specific site faults the teardown finds, phrased as one-line
  hooks.
- **The urgency hook:** the act-now reason as one sentence.
- **The two objection pre-empts** most likely in this niche, with the one-line counter each.

## Hard lines
- **No invented economics.** If the ticket size or the fault pattern is a guess, mark it as
  an assumption to verify, don't state it as fact. A wedge built on a wrong number collapses
  on the first call.
- **Every price routes to the tree.** Positioning names a tier, not a made-up number.
  PROPOSED-v2 SKUs stay flagged, never quoted as final.
- **Compliance rails travel with the niche.** Regulated verticals (medical, legal, finance)
  carry claim limits. Name them so the copy doesn't overreach. (Medspa: no medical outcome
  claims, signed-consent galleries.)
- **One niche per run.** Don't blur two verticals into a mushy "med and wellness" pitch.

## Voice (hard rules)
No em-dashes or en-dashes, ever. Short sentences, 9-13 words. Contractions always. The
building blocks read like an operator who studied the business, not a marketer guessing.
Numbers do the talking. Full spec: `~/Claude/business-library/VOICE-SPEC.md`.

## Output structure (produce this)
1. **The wedge sentence.**
2. **The four inputs**, filled (flag any assumption).
3. **The messaging spine:** diagnosis flip, tier steer, three proof angles, urgency hook,
   two objection pre-empts.
4. **One honest gap:** what [OWNER] doesn't yet know about this niche and should confirm before
   heavy outreach.

## WORKED EXAMPLE — men's-health / GLP-1 clinics
*Input: [OWNER] wants to open men's-health and weight-loss clinics (TRT, GLP-1 weight loss, ED,
longevity) as a niche adjacent to the medspa beachhead. Known: high-ticket recurring
memberships, telehealth-heavy, aggressive growth, sites that look like pill-mill landing
pages or generic telehealth templates.*

**The wedge:** They sell a discreet, ongoing medical membership, and their sites either look
like a sketchy supplement funnel or a faceless telehealth template, so trust leaks before the
first booking.

**The four inputs:**
1. *How they make money:* recurring memberships, GLP-1 programs [REFERRAL_FEE]-500/mo, TRT protocols
   $100-300/mo, often 6-12 month commitments. A retained patient is worth $1,500-5,000/yr.
   (Economics modeled on the medspa GLP-1 line; *verify actual ticket ranges for men's-health
   specifically before quoting.*)
2. *Who signs the check:* owner-physician or clinic operator. Wants patient volume and
   membership retention, wary of anything that reads "spammy" because the category already
   fights a credibility problem. Talk conversions and trust, not brand.
3. *What their site does wrong:* no real faces or credentials (patients vet who's prescribing),
   booking buried behind an intake wall, drug brand names splashed where platform policy will
   punish them, no membership page. (*Assumption from category norms and the medspa teardown
   pattern; confirm with 2-3 real teardowns.*)
4. *The urgency line:* the GLP-1 goldrush. Weight-loss memberships are the fastest-growing
   revenue line in the category right now, and every clinic is racing for the landing page.

**The messaging spine:**
- *Diagnosis flip:* "You don't have a traffic problem. You have a trust problem. Your site
  looks like a supplement ad, and men buying a medical membership need to see a real clinic."
- *Tier steer:* booking is the business and memberships need dedicated pages, so E-com/Booking
  [ECOM_PRICE] is the floor; a dead or template-only site goes White-Glove [WHITE_GLOVE]. Route to
  `pricing-tree.md`, no custom number.
- *Three proof angles:* (1) "No prescriber's face or license visible, in a category built on
  trust." (2) "Booking sits behind a wall instead of above the fold." (3) "The membership,
  your whole recurring revenue, gets one paragraph instead of a page."
- *Urgency hook:* "GLP-1 demand is peaking and your competitor already has the landing page.
  This is the quarter that page pays for itself."
- *Objection pre-empts:* "We run telehealth software already" -> "I don't replace it, I embed
  it and build the trust layer around it." "We tried an agency" -> "You paid for reports. This
  is a fixed-price site that books patients, then optional care. No retainer."

**One honest gap:** the real ticket sizes, commitment lengths, and platform-policy landmines
(pharmacy advertising rules, drug-name restrictions) for men's-health specifically are modeled
off the medspa line and need confirming with two or three real teardowns and one owner
conversation before [OWNER] runs heavy outreach. The wedge holds; the numbers need a check.
