# agency-partner-pitch

## When to use
Pitching a specific agency on white-label web fulfillment: a one-pager to attach, deck
content, or the outreach email. Input: the agency (size, what they sell, one REAL
observed detail from their site or portfolio). Output: the one-pager content and/or
the email, ready for [OWNER]'s click. No observed detail means go get one first; a
pitch with no evidence of their bottleneck is spam. This is the core offer, warm
routes beat cold (icp-and-personas.md).

## The argument, in order (one-pager and deck follow this spine)
1. **The diagnosis flip (headline).** "Most agencies don't have a sales problem. They
   have a website fulfillment bottleneck." Their pain first, never [OWNER]'s bio first.
2. **The offer, flat.** "[FIRST_BUILD] flat, first build. White-label custom sites under your
   brand. That's the offer." Then the rate card after build one: Landing [LANDING_PAGE],
   Standard [STANDARD_SITE], Booking/E-com [ECOM_PRICE]. At 3+ builds a month: 10% partner pricing,
   floors $700 landing and [FIRST_BUILD] standard.
3. **The margin math, from THEIR side.** They bill their client $3,000-5,000 per
   site (most partners charge 2 to 3x). My invoice is [STANDARD_SITE] standard. Their margin:
   [PROJECT_EXAMPLE]-3,800 per site with no hire, no payroll, no babysitting. First build: bill
   $3,000, pay [FIRST_BUILD], judge the work with $2,000 still in their pocket.
4. **How it runs.** Send the scope. 50% books the slot. First draft in 48-72 hours.
   Day-3 preview they can forward to their client. First build: unlimited revisions
   until their client approves. Delivered under their brand, written scope so their
   client can't creep on them.
5. **The invisibility rules, unprompted.** NDA standard. Their brand on everything.
   They own all client comms; I never contact their client, ever. Say this before
   they have to ask, it's the fear that kills these deals (icp objection #2).
6. **Why not Fiverr.** The COO wedge: ran ops inside a marketing agency as fractional
   COO, scaled it [PRIOR_RESULT] a year. Six years, 35+ white-label builds. "I know
   what a client breathing down your neck feels like. Fiverr can't say that."
7. **Capacity + the close.** Taking 3 agency partners this quarter (verify against
   offers.md first). "Send me the scope. I'll tell you if I can help."

## Pricing note (do not skip)
After the first build the RATE CARD applies: [STANDARD_SITE] standard. offers.md carries a
TODO on whether the ongoing rate drops to [FIRST_BUILD]. Until [OWNER] confirms, quote the
rate card for build two onward and never promise [FIRST_BUILD] past build one.

## The email version (hard limits)
Under 90 words. Opens on THEIR observed detail, not on [OWNER]. One price ([FIRST_BUILD] first
build), one proof point, the invisibility line, the scope-filter close. One link max.
Sign "[OWNER]" alone. The one-pager attaches or follows on reply, the email never
summarizes all seven sections.

## Voice (hard rules)
No em-dashes or en-dashes, ever. Operator to operator: pipeline, fulfillment, margin,
markup, bench, shipped. Short sentences. Numbers do the talking. Pipes in headline
copy only. Never "partnership opportunity," never "synergy," never a compliment
sandwich. Full spec: `business-library/VOICE-SPEC.md`.

## True facts you may state
Six years. Fractional COO, [PRIOR_RESULT] per year. 35+ white-label builds. 48-72 hr
first drafts, day-3 forwardable preview, live in 7 days. NDA standard. Unlimited
revisions on the first build until their client approves. Never name other agency
clients, never invent volume or logos.

## WORKED EXAMPLE: pitch to a 5-person brand agency
*Input: Foundry & Co, 5-person brand studio, Portland. Identity and packaging work.
Observed: their portfolio shows the Meridian Coffee rebrand, and the "visit site"
link goes to a default-template Squarespace page that doesn't match the identity.*

**1. The email:**

> Subject: the Meridian site
>
> Nate, the Meridian rebrand on your site is sharp work. Then the "visit site" link
> lands on a template Squarespace page that doesn't match the identity you built.
>
> That's not a design problem, it's a fulfillment bottleneck, and it's the one thing
> I fix. White-label builds under your brand: first one is [FIRST_BUILD] flat, draft in
> 48-72 hours, revisions until your client approves, NDA standard. You bill your
> client whatever you bill, most partners charge 2 to 3x.
>
> Send me one scope. I'll tell you if I can help.
>
> [OWNER]

**2. The one-pager (content, built on the spine):**

> **Most agencies don't have a sales problem. They have a website fulfillment
> bottleneck.**
>
> You design identities clients love, then the website ships late, off-brand, or not
> at all. The build muscle is the missing piece, and hiring for it doesn't pencil at
> five people.
>
> **The offer.** [FIRST_BUILD] flat, first build. White-label custom sites under your brand.
> That's the offer. After the first: [LANDING_PAGE] landing | [STANDARD_SITE] standard | [ECOM_PRICE] booking
> or e-com. Three or more builds a month earns 10% partner pricing.
>
> **Your math, not mine.** You bill your client $3,000-5,000 for the site that ships
> with the rebrand. My invoice is [STANDARD_SITE]. You keep [PROJECT_EXAMPLE]-3,800 per site without
> hiring anyone. On the first build you pay [FIRST_BUILD] and judge the work with your
> margin already banked.
>
> **How it runs.** Send the scope. 50% books the slot. First draft in 48-72 hours,
> and a day-3 preview you can forward straight to your client. First build carries
> unlimited revisions until your client signs off. Delivered under your brand with a
> written scope, so your client can't creep on you.
>
> **The rules that protect you.** NDA standard. Your brand on everything. You own
> every client conversation, I never contact your client. I built this model on
> staying invisible, it's the product.
>
> **Why me and not Fiverr.** I ran ops inside a marketing agency as fractional COO
> and scaled it [PRIOR_RESULT] a year. Six years, 35+ white-label builds. I know what
> a client breathing down your neck feels like. Fiverr can't say that.
>
> **Taking 3 agency partners this quarter.** Send me the scope on whatever's most
> stuck. I'll tell you if I can help.

Why it works: the email spends its first line proving [OWNER] actually looked, and the
bottleneck names itself. The one-pager sells margin, not websites: every number is
theirs (what they bill, what they keep) except the four that are his rate card. The
invisibility section arrives before the fear does. And the close is the scope filter,
which reads as confidence because saying no is on the table.
