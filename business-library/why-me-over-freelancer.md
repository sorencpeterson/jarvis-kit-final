## The seven reasons, each with its proof

**1. I've sat in your chair.** Fractional COO inside a marketing agency, [PRIOR_BASELINE] to
$1M+ per year, churn cut from an account every 2-3 months to 2+ year retention
(`~/Claude/second-brain/store/case/400k-to-1m.html`). A freelancer optimizes for
the ticket in front of them. I price in the thing the ticket never mentions: your
client's patience.

**2. Reliability is a system, not a personality trait.** The [OWNER] Method
(`[OWNER_HANDLE]-method.md`): an intake gate means the build doesn't start until every
asset is in hand, so nothing stalls mid-build waiting on a logo. Scripted convert,
import, and QA stages mean the 40th build runs like the 4th. A freelancer's
reliability lives in their mood and their week. Mine is written down and gated.

**3. End to end means end to end.** Design, development, copy, QA, go-live, and
the care plan after (`sops/deliver-a-site.md`). With a cheap freelancer, you're
still the project manager: specs, chasing, midnight fixes. The management tax is
the real price, and it's paid in the only thing you're actually out of.

**4. Invisibility is policy, not a favor.** NDA standard (offers.md). Your brand
on everything. A written confidentiality policy, and I never contact your client,
ever (`sops/deliver-a-site.md`, white-label variant). The delivery email arrives
client-ready so you can forward it as your own. Your client never learns I exist.
"You look great, I stay out of it" (`icp-and-personas.md`, objection 2 counter).

**5. The risk sits with me first.** First build [FIRST_BUILD] flat so you judge the work
with real stakes (pricing-tree). 50% deposit, 50% on delivery, so disappearing
means disappearing unpaid (`playbooks/objections.md` #26). Unlimited revisions on
that first build until your client approves (offers.md guarantee). Day-3 preview
you can forward before anything goes live. A [REFERRAL_FEE] gig asks you to carry all of
that risk yourself.

**6. Speed that's honest.** White-label builds land in 48-72 hours from complete
assets (offers.md), and the intake gate is why that number is real instead of
brave: the clock starts when everything's in hand, so there's no mid-build stall
(`sops/deliver-a-site.md` Stage 0). Fast from a system, not fast from cutting QA.

**7. The margin math works on your side.** [FIRST_BUILD]-1,200 my side, [ECOM_PRICE]-5,000
resale your side (`~/Claude/MONEY-THIS-MONTH.md` §4). Most partners charge 2-3x
(`playbooks/sales-calls.md` ICP B). At 3 builds a month you're clearing four
figures of margin without hiring anyone, and 3+ orders/mo earns partner pricing
(pricing-tree).

## What the freelancer honestly keeps
Say this unprompted. It buys more trust than any claim.
- **Price.** They win it, permanently. If price is the only column that matters,
  they win, and that's ok (`playbooks/objections.md` #34).
- **Fine for throwaway work.** A placeholder page with no client attached? Take
  the [REFERRAL_FEE] route, seriously. The wedge only matters when your name is on the
  result and a relationship is on the line.
- **Fine at volume with real PM.** A shop with a dedicated project manager and a
  spec library can run offshore capacity well. If that's you, I'm your overflow
  lane, not your replacement.

## Usage rules
1. Concede price in the first breath. Never argue it. The wedge starts where price
   ends.
2. One receipt beats three claims. Prefer Monday-Thursday, the [STANDARD_SITE] Acme Co
   close, or the case study numbers over any adjective
   (`proof-receipts-library.md`).
3. Say the counter, then stop talking (`playbooks/objections.md` header rule).
4. If they're genuinely better served by a freelancer, say so and leave the door
   open. Half of these come back (objections #19).
