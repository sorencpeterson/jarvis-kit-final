# Comment Strategy System: C50

Source: 250-IDEAS-BUSINESS.md C50. 15 minutes a day on 5 target agencies'/owners'
posts. The daily system, plus 10 example comments on hypothetical posts (illustrative
only, no real posts referenced, since these are hypothetical targets).

**Status: this is a daily habit system, not an automated send.** Comments are
[OWNER]'s own words, picked from candidates, posted by him personally. Nothing here
auto-posts.

---

## The daily system (15 minutes)
1. **Pick 5 target accounts**: a short, fixed list of agency owners or local
   business owners whose posts are worth engaging (not random scrolling). Revisit
   the list monthly, swap out anyone who's gone quiet or isn't a fit anymore.
2. **Check each of the 5, once a day.** Skip if nothing new. Don't hunt for content
   to comment on, only comment on what's actually there.
3. **Draft 2-3 comment candidates per relevant post**, pulled from the actual text
   of the post (not generic). Pick the one that's true and adds something, not the
   one that's just agreeable.
4. **Post it as [OWNER], in [OWNER]'s voice.** No auto-posting. This is relationship
   work, not distribution.
5. **Cap: 5-10 comments a day total** across all 5 accounts combined. Quality over
   volume, this isn't an engagement farm.

## What makes a good comment (the filter)
- Adds a real opinion, a number, or a specific detail. Never just "great post" or
  "so true."
- Short. 1-3 sentences most of the time. A comment isn't a second post.
- No pitch, no link, no "check out my thing." If it reads like an ad, it's not a
  comment, it's spam with a shorter word count.
- Occasionally disagrees, respectfully, with a reason. Total agreement every time
  reads as flattery, not engagement.

## 10 example comments (on hypothetical posts, illustrative only)

**On a post about agencies struggling to hire developers:**
"The hire isn't the fix most of the time. It's usually a documentation problem
first, nobody can onboard fast because nothing's written down. Fixed that before I
fixed headcount."

**On a post celebrating a big client win:**
"Congrats, that's a real one. Curious what actually moved the needle, was it the
pitch or something in the delivery process that made the close easier?"

**On a post complaining about slow web dev turnaround:**
"48-72 hours is achievable if the process is built for it, most delays are actually
scope creep mid-build, not raw dev time. Worth auditing where the days are actually
going."

**On a post about a local business owner's slow month:**
"Slow months are usually when the fixable stuff gets found. Worth a full look at
the site while there's time to actually act on it instead of just riding it out."

**On a post sharing a before/after design transformation:**
"The 'after' looks clean, but the real test is whether the contact form actually
works and loads fast on a phone. Design's half the job."

**On a post about AI replacing web developers:**
"AI changed how fast I deliver, not what I'm actually selling. The judgment on what
to fix and why is still the job. Tools change, that part hasn't."

**On a post about an agency owner burning out:**
"Burnout's usually a systems gap wearing a person costume. If everything's still in
your head, you're the bottleneck by design, not by accident."

**On a post asking for website feedback:**
"Ran your homepage through a quick mental checklist: mobile load looked slow and I
didn't see a clear single next step above the fold. Both fixable, happy to point to
specifics if useful."

**On a post about a failed marketing campaign:**
"The campaign probably wasn't the failure. Worth checking what happened after the
click, a lot of 'the ad didn't work' turns out to be 'the landing page leaked
everyone who clicked it.'"

**On a post about referral-based growth:**
"Referrals convert better because the trust transfer already happened before the
first conversation. Worth building a system around asking for them on purpose
instead of hoping they show up."
