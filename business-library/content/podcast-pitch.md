## 10-SHOW TARGET CRITERIA
A show is worth pitching if it hits at least 3 of these:
1. Audience is agency owners or local-service business owners (the actual ICP).
2. Show has covered operations, systems, or scaling topics before (not pure
   marketing-tactics content).
3. Host actively interviews guests (not a solo-format show).
4. Recent episodes (last 90 days) show the host still actively publishing.
5. Episode length runs 25+ minutes (room for the actual story, not a 5-minute
   segment).
6. Show has a visible audience (reviews, social following, or download numbers if
   published), not a brand-new show with zero traction, unless the host is
   otherwise well-connected.
7. Past guests are a believable peer set (other operators, agency owners, not
   celebrities or unrelated niches).
8. Host's own content voice is direct and non-hype (a fit-check on tone before
   committing to an hour on their show).
9. Show is discoverable in podcast search for terms like "agency growth,"
   "marketing operations," or "small business systems."
10. No overlap with a show already pitched or booked in the current quarter
    (spread the story out, don't burn it across near-identical audiences at once).

## THE 3 STORIES [OWNER] TELLS
Pick based on what the host's audience actually needs; don't tell all three on
every show.

**Story 1: The board fix.** Everything lived in the founder's head. Pulling it into
one shared, visible system was the first move, and it's the most universally
applicable story (works for any audience with an overwhelmed owner).

**Story 2: The white-label wedge.** How white-label site fulfillment for agencies
works as a business, the actual mechanics of staying invisible to the end client,
NDA standard, and why that model works for agencies with a fulfillment bottleneck
specifically. Best for agency-owner-heavy audiences.

**Story 3: The system-over-hustle case.** Revenue went from [PRIOR_BASELINE] to over $1M not
by working more hours, by building the system that let the owner work fewer. Churn
dropped from an account lost every 2-3 months to average retention past two years.
Best for general small-business or founder-burnout-adjacent audiences.
