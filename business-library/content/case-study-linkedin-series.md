## PART 1: The setup (the problem, not the solution yet)
The founder was the business. That was the actual problem.

Single owner, about [PRIOR_BASELINE] a year, one or two employees, a growing roster of overseas
freelancers who were making things worse, not better, because there was nothing to
hand them.

Tasks lived in his head. Deliverables slipped. Clients went quiet because nobody was
sending updates. An account churned every two to three months. He was working more
hours than anyone should and the agency was still fragile.

He brought me in to fix the backend, end to end. This is the first of three moves
that actually mattered. Part 2 next week.

## PART 2: Move 1: everything out of his head
First move: pulled every client, every deliverable, every deadline out of his head
and into one shared board.

Status stopped being a conversation you had to have. It became something you could
just look at.

That single change stopped deliverables from slipping through the cracks and gave
clients a reason to stop worrying. It sounds almost too simple to be the first real
fix. It was.

Part 3 next week: the system that became the whole operating backbone.

## PART 3: Move 2: one system, not a stack
Second move: made one platform the operating system for the entire agency, not just
a CRM.

Booking and intake for the agency's own pipeline. White-labeled so the agency looked
like a full-stack operation to its clients. Automated fulfillment handoffs so work
moved forward without the founder pushing every step by hand.

The agency started running like a machine instead of running through a person.

Part 4: the hardest move, building a team around it.

## PART 4: Move 3: the team, built around real systems
Third move: recruited the key hires, including vetted overseas talent, and gave each
one documented procedures and a live system to step into instead of a pile of
tribal knowledge.

Layered automation onto the repetitive production and reporting work so the team
spent their time on the parts that actually needed a human.

The founder went from inside every task to genuinely hands-off. Not "less involved."
Hands-off.

Last part next week: the actual numbers.
