## The core problem this call solves
These people raised a hand about growing their business, not about a website.
They came in on [SECOND_BRAND], booked a call, and nobody ever connected, most
of them 4 to 9 months ago. So this is not a website pitch and it is not a cold
call. It is a re-open. The goal of the FIRST touch is not to close and not to
pitch. It is to get them back on the phone and find out what they actually need
now. The offer comes later, after the need is clear, and it is whatever fits.

That is the whole discipline: **re-open vague, diagnose live, pivot to the
offer that fits.** Pitching an offer before you know the need is how these calls
died the first time.

## The five stages

### Stage 1: The re-open (30-45 sec)
Own the drop, make it human, ask if the need is still live.
- **Own the miss.** "We had a call on the books a while back and somewhere along
  the way we never connected. That one is on me." Taking the blame disarms the
  awkwardness of a months-old no-show and makes you the reasonable one.
- **Give a reason for calling now.** "Your name came up this week, so I figured
  I would reach out direct instead of playing phone tag." A reason beats a
  cold-open. It says this is not a blast.
- **Ask the one vague qualifying question.** Swap the noun to the niche:
  - Clinics and med spas: "Are you still looking to get more patients booked, or
    did you get that handled?"
  - Contractors (HVAC, plumbing, electrical): "Are you still looking to get more
    jobs on the calendar, or did you get that sorted?"
  - Salons: "Are you still looking to keep the chairs full, or is that handled?"
  - Default: "Are you still looking to get more customers in the door, or did you
    get that handled?"
- **The "or did you get that handled" is doing real work.** It gives them an easy
  out, which makes the yes real. A hand-raise you gave them permission to decline
  is a warm lead. Do not skip the out.

Rule: no pitch in stage 1. None. You are opening a door, not walking through it.

### Stage 2: Need-finding (the heart of the call, 5-8 min)
This is where a vague re-open becomes a real conversation. Do not steer toward a
website. Steer toward their pain and let the offer reveal itself.
- **Start wide.** "Walk me through where things are at right now with getting new
  customers." Then shut up and listen. The first thing they complain about is
  usually the real problem.
- **Get the anchor number, in their language.** By niche:
  - Clinic/med spa: "What is a patient worth to you over the life of a program?"
  - HVAC/contractor: "What is an average job worth to you?"
  - Salon: "What is a regular client worth over a year, and how many no-shows do
    you eat in a week?"
  The anchor number, out of their mouth, is what every later offer prices
  against. Write it down. Say it back.
- **Find where they leak.** "Where do new customers come from now, and what
  happens to someone who finds you at 9pm?" This surfaces the gap without naming
  a solution yet. Their answer tells you if it is a website problem, a booking
  problem, a follow-up problem, or a "they never call back" problem.
- **Name the last thing they tried.** "What did the last marketing spend
  actually produce that you could count?" Most of them were burned by a retainer
  that shipped reports, not customers. That burn is your positioning: fixed
  price, countable outcome, anti-retainer.
- Rule: numbers and pain out of THEIR mouth. You are diagnosing, not presenting.
  The person who talks least in stage 2 wins the call.

### Stage 3: The pivot to an offer (2-4 min)
Now, and only now, connect their stated need to a specific offer from the ladder.
The pivot is a bridge from their words to your SKU, not a menu.
- **Reflect the need, then name the fit.** "So you told me a patient is worth
  {number} and you are losing the 9pm booker because the site cannot take the
  appointment. The fix for that is a booking site. That is {price}." Their
  problem, then your one offer, then the price. No buffet.
- **Route by what they actually said** (full logic in pricing-tree.md and the
  niche books):
  - Losing bookings, weak or dead site -> Booking [ECOM_PRICE], or White-Glove [WHITE_GLOVE]
    if they need copy/brand/photos, not a reskin.
  - Site is fine but leaks or loads slow -> Webfix [WEBFIX], or a booking-focused
    fix.
  - No site at all, needs to move fast -> Landing [LANDING_PAGE] to start, Standard the
    follow-on.
  - Local service, standard need -> Standard [STANDARD_SITE], Booking [ECOM_PRICE] if they take
    appointments.
  - It turns out they are actually an agency or an owner buried in delivery ->
    that is the white-label or COO lane, not this local motion. Switch scripts.
- **Anchor the price to their number.** "Based on the {job/patient/appointment
  value} you gave me, this pays for itself in {n}." Always their number, never a
  generic ROI line.
- **Then silence.** State the price once and stop talking. The first one to speak
  owns the objection. This is the hardest and most important move on the call.

### Stage 4: Handle and close (2-3 min)
- **If they push back,** pull the counter from objections-local-biz.md (the
  local-biz set) or the 50-item bank, say it, and stop. Ask for the close once
  more. Do not stack three counters, one clean counter then silence.
- **On a yes:** "Two things. Tap the deposit link, then send me your logo. Half
  now, half when it is live in 7 days, you approve the preview on day 3." Never
  let a yes float. Concrete next steps in one breath.
- **On a maybe:** name the real objection, handle it once, then set a hard date.
  "The price holds 14 days, then it is a requote. Grab a slot this week and we
  are done in 15 minutes." Never end a call without a date: deposit date,
  decision date, or check-back date.

### Stage 5: The no-answer and the no
- **No answer:** leave the voicemail (MONEY-THIS-MONTH.md Section 2), then send
  the text. Voicemail plus text beats either alone. Drive to [OWNER_SITE]/book.
- **A clean no:** "No worries at all, I will close it out on my end. If anything
  changes, my name finds easy." Instant, clean, zero guilt. Tag it and move on.
- **A "not right now":** "What changes in a few months? If the answer is nothing,
  the timing is really something else. What is it?" Then either surface the real
  objection or set a real check-back date.

## Universal rules for this call
- **Re-open vague, always.** The first touch is a door, not a pitch. Any offer in
  the opener kills the warm re-engagement.
- **Their number, said back to them, closes harder than any pitch line.** Get it
  in stage 2, price against it in stage 3.
- **Every price is followed by silence.** Count to five in your head.
- **Never end without a date.** Deposit, decision, or check-back.
- **Log the objection you heard, verbatim, right after the call.** The library
  and the objection bank learn from real ones.
- **These warm leads are NOT the agency or COO scripts.** They are local business
  owners who came in on [SECOND_BRAND]. If the call reveals they are actually an
  agency or an owner buried in delivery, that is a different lane, switch to the
  white-label or COO framing. Otherwise stay in this local motion.

## Why this is the week-1 motion
The fastest dollars are not new pitches. They are the 58 people who already
booked a call and never closed, plus the 423 who replied. Even a 5-10% re-open
rate is several live conversations, and the month comes down to 3-4 closed
projects. That makes hitting the number a follow-up problem, not a lead problem.
This framework is how the follow-up gets worked. Warmest money first, vague
re-open, diagnose live, pivot to what fits.
