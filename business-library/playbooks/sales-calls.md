# Sales Call Playbooks — per ICP
_Loaded by the call coach (alongside store/call_frameworks.md) and JARVIS. Two ICPs,
two different calls. Stages match the coach's 1-5 bar. No em-dashes._

## ICP A: Local service owner (HVAC, plumbing, roofing, trades)
Who they are: owner-operator, phone in a truck, hates meetings, respects trades.
Talk like a contractor, not a marketer. Price in jobs, not dollars.

**Stage 1, Open (30 sec):** "Hey {name}, [OWNER]. You booked a call about the site
{context}. I'll keep this to 15 minutes. Before I talk: what made you book?"
Rule: their reason first. It's the whole call's steering wheel.

**Stage 2, Dig (5 min):** Three questions, in order:
1. "What's an average job worth to you?" (sets the anchor for everything)
2. "Where do customers come from right now, and what happens when they hit your site?"
3. "If the phone rang twice more a week, what would that change?"
Rule: numbers out of THEIR mouth. Write them down, say them back.

**Stage 3, Show (3 min):** Walk one or two faults from their teardown. "Your site
does X. A customer at 9pm with a burst pipe sees Y. They call the next guy."
Rule: one fault fully understood beats five listed.

**Stage 4, Price (2 min):** "{tier} is {price}. Half now, half when it's live in 7
days. You approve the preview on day 3. Based on your {job value}, this pays for
itself in {n} jobs." Then silence. Count to five in your head.

**Stage 5, Close:** "Want it handled?" If yes: deposit link + logo, today. If maybe:
name the objection, use the playbook, ask again ONCE, then set a 48h deadline.

## ICP B: Agency owner (white-label)
Who they are: sold sites they can't build, margin-squeezed, terrified of vendors
who embarrass them in front of clients. Buying reliability, not creativity.

**Stage 1, Open:** "You sell the site, I build it, your name stays on it. First one
is [FIRST_BUILD] flat so you can judge the work with real stakes. What's on your bench
right now?"

**Stage 2, Dig:** 1. "How many builds a month do you sell?" 2. "What kills your
margin today: build time, revisions, or the client swirl?" 3. "What did your last
vendor get wrong?"
Rule: the last-vendor answer is the spec for how to win them.

**Stage 3, Show:** delivery machine, not design: 7-day turnaround, day-3 preview
THEY can forward to their client, two revision rounds, white-label everything,
written scope so their client can't creep on them.

**Stage 4, Price:** "[FIRST_BUILD] first build. After that, rate card: 800 landing, 1,200
standard, 2,500 booking. You mark up whatever you want, most partners charge 2 to
3x. At 3 builds a month you're clearing four figures of margin without hiring."

**Stage 5, Close:** "Send me one real order this week. If the delivery isn't the
easiest part of your month, we never talk again."

## Universal rules (both ICPs)
- Their number, said back to them, closes harder than any pitch line.
- Every price is followed by silence. The first one to speak owns the objection.
- Never end without a date: deposit date, decision date, or check-back date.
- Log the objection you heard, verbatim, right after the call. The library learns.
- Booked call no-show: text within 5 minutes: "No stress. Rebook here when ready:
  {book link}. The plan I built you is still live." Then the 43-style loop-close in 3 days.
