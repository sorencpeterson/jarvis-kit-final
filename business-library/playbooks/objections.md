# Objection Playbook — 50 counters in [OWNER]'s voice
_Loaded by the call coach, the reply concierge, and JARVIS. Format: what they say,
what you say, why it works. Say the counter, then STOP TALKING. Silence closes.
No em-dashes anywhere. These are word-for-word usable._

## PRICE (1-12)

**1. "That's too expensive."**
Say: "Compared to what? One missed job a month costs you more than this site does. What's an average job worth for you?"
Why: makes them price the loss, not the site. Their number does the selling.

**2. "I can get a site for [REFERRAL_FEE] on Fiverr."**
Say: "You can. You'll get a template with your name on it. This is built off what's actually costing you customers, you saw the five faults. Templates can't see."
Why: you already showed the diagnosis. Contrast evidence vs commodity.

**3. "I don't have the budget right now."**
Say: "Fair. The deposit is half, and it holds this month's price for 14 days. When does budget open up? I'll time it to that."
Why: converts a no into a scheduled yes and keeps the price anchor alive.

**4. "Can you do it cheaper?"**
Say: "The price is the price. What I can do is trade: knock 10 percent off for a testimonial on delivery and two intros to people like you. Deal?"
Why: rule 3, never discount for silence. A discount must buy something.

**5. "Why is it [STANDARD_SITE] when hosting is $10 a month?"**
Say: "Hosting is the parking spot. This is the car. The [STANDARD_SITE] is the part that makes the phone ring."
Why: category correction, short and visual.

**6. "Your competitor quoted me $600."**
Say: "Take it if you trust it. Ask them to walk you through five specific faults on your current site first. That's the difference you're paying for."
Why: confidence plus a test the cheap guy fails.

**7. "What if it doesn't work?"**
Say: "Define work. You'll see the working preview on day 3 and approve before anything goes live. You're never buying blind."
Why: kills risk with process, not promises.

**8. "Can we do payment plans?"**
Say: "50 percent to start, 50 on delivery. That IS the payment plan, and delivery is a week away."
Why: reframes existing terms as the accommodation.

**9. "I need to think about the money."**
Say: "Sure. While you think: the price holds 14 days, then it's requoted. What number would make this a yes today?"
Why: deadline plus a direct ask that surfaces the real objection.

**10. "We just spent our budget on ads."**
Say: "Then you're paying to send people to a site that leaks them. Fix the bucket before you buy more water."
Why: their spend becomes your argument.

**11. "Is there a cheaper version?"**
Say: "Yes, the [LANDING_PAGE] landing page. One page, one goal. Half my clients start there and upgrade once it's earning."
Why: real downsell path beats a discount. Protects the ladder.

**12. "Money's tight this season."**
Say: "That's exactly when the phone needs to ring more, not less. Slow season is when you build so busy season pays for it."
Why: seasonality flipped into urgency.

## TIMING (13-20)

**13. "Not right now / maybe next quarter."**
Say: "Ok. What changes next quarter? If the answer is nothing, the timing objection is really something else. What is it?"
Why: politely names the dodge. Often unlocks the real objection.

**14. "Call me back in a few months."**
Say: "I will. Meanwhile the proposal link stays live 14 days at this price. After that it's a requote. Want me to hold a build slot instead?"
Why: scarcity that's true (slots are real capacity).

**15. "I'm too busy to deal with this."**
Say: "You deal with it twice: 15 minutes now, 20 minutes on day 3 to approve. I handle everything else. That's the whole ask."
Why: quantifies THEIR time. Busy people buy handled.

**16. "We're in our busy season."**
Say: "Perfect. Your busy season traffic is exactly what the new site should be catching. Every week it waits is your best-traffic week wasted."
Why: their calendar argues for you.

**17. "Let me get through the holidays."**
Say: "Build takes 7 days. Start now and you come out of the holidays with it live, instead of starting from zero in January like everyone else."
Why: paints the after-state on their timeline.

**18. "How fast can you really do it?"**
Say: "Standard is 7 days from deposit. Need it faster? Rush is 3 days for 50 percent more. Most people don't need rush."
Why: rule 2 stated plainly, and the last line builds trust by unselling.

**19. "We already started with someone else."**
Say: "Then finish with them, seriously. If it stalls, my proposal's live for two weeks. Keep it as your plan B."
Why: graceful exit that leaves the door open. Half of these come back.

**20. "Send me info and I'll look later."**
Say: "Already did, that's the proposal link. It's a 2-minute read. Read it now while I'm on the line and tell me what's wrong with it."
Why: 'later' is where deals die. Compress to now.

## TRUST & PROOF (21-28)

**21. "How do I know you're legit?"**
Say: "You don't yet. That's why you approve a working preview on day 3 before the balance. And I took a client from 400K to a million a year, I'll walk you through how."
Why: process safety plus the one number that matters.

**22. "Do you have reviews / examples?"**
Say: "Yes, I'll text you two live sites and the case study. While you look: what would YOUR site need to do for this to be a win?"
Why: gives proof and immediately re-engages on their goal.

**23. "You're not local."**
Say: "Your customers find you on Google, not by driving past my office. I build for how they actually buy. And you can call me directly, that part is very local."
Why: relocates 'local' to what matters.

**24. "How long have you been doing this?"**
Say: "Six years. The last agency I ran ops for went from 400K to a million a year on systems I built. I'd rather show you than tell you, got 15 minutes?"
Why: honest number, then straight to proof-by-walkthrough.

**25. "Are you AI? / Did AI write this?"**
Say: "I use AI in my delivery stack, that's why I'm fast and why the price is what it is. The judgment and the guarantee are me. You're talking to [OWNER]."
Why: honest, turns the tool into the advantage. Never lie about this.

**26. "What if you disappear after I pay?"**
Say: "Half up front, half on delivery. If I disappear, I disappear unpaid, and my name is on my own website. The incentives are aligned."
Why: structure answers it better than reassurance.

**27. "I got burned by the last web guy."**
Say: "Then you know exactly what to check for. Day 3 preview, approve before live, everything in writing on one page. Hold me to all of it."
Why: welcomes scrutiny instead of dodging the wound.

**28. "Why should I trust a proposal a robot made?"**
Say: "The five faults in it are from your actual site. Check any of them. If one's wrong, hang up on me."
Why: evidence invites verification. Verification builds trust.

## THE NEPHEW & COMPETITORS (29-34)

**29. "My nephew/friend does websites."**
Say: "Great, this proposal doubles as his spec sheet. If he can hit it at this price, hire him. If he's still 'getting to it' in two weeks, call me."
Why: zero defensiveness. Time does the work.

**30. "We have an agency already."**
Say: "Keep them. I white-label for agencies like yours, ask them what they'd charge for this scope and compare. Sometimes I AM their vendor."
Why: true and disarming. Opens the b2b door.

**31. "Wix says I can do it myself free."**
Say: "You can, and you'll do it at 11pm after work, for months. Your hourly rate says that's the most expensive site you could build."
Why: their time priced honestly.

**32. "The other quote includes SEO forever."**
Say: "'SEO forever' for a flat fee usually means a monthly report nobody reads. Ask them what specifically they do each month. Then ask me the same. Compare answers."
Why: teaches the test instead of trashing the rival.

**33. "I found someone on Facebook for half."**
Say: "Screenshot their proposal and mine side by side. If theirs names one specific fault on your site, take it."
Why: the diagnosis is the moat. Use it.

**34. "Can you match their price?"**
Say: "No. I can beat their scope, their timeline, and their proof. If price is the only column that matters, they win, and that's ok."
Why: walking away from price-only buyers is rule 7 in action.

## DIY / TEMPLATE / SCOPE (35-42)

**35. "Is this just a template?"**
Say: "The tech is standard, the build is yours. Nobody else gets your diagnosis, your copy, your call flow. Templates don't know your break-even on a job."
Why: separates commodity parts from custom value.

**36. "Can you just fix my current site instead?"**
Say: "If it's salvageable, yes, that's the [WEBFIX] fix bundle. But your teardown found structural problems. Fixing paint on a cracked wall wastes your [WEBFIX]."
Why: honest routing per the pricing tree. Downsell when true, not when pressured.

**37. "Can we add a blog / booking / store later?"**
Say: "Yes, that's the next tier up, and the build is structured so upgrading doesn't mean rebuilding. Start where the money is: calls."
Why: protects scope now, seeds the upgrade.

**38. "While you're in there, can you also..."**
Say: "That's outside this scope, and I protect your price by protecting the scope. I'll quote it as an add-on after delivery."
Why: scope creep line, verbatim from the pricing rules.

**39. "I want to write my own copy."**
Say: "Deal. You write it, I'll edit it for conversion. If it's not in by day 2, my draft goes in so we ship on time, you can swap yours in a revision."
Why: keeps the timeline out of their hands without a fight.

**40. "Can I see designs from three options first?"**
Say: "No, spec work is how sites end up committee-flavored. You get a working preview on day 3 and two revision rounds. That's better than mockups: it's real."
Why: rule 5. Sells the preview as superior, which it is.

**41. "It needs to look exactly like [huge brand]'s site."**
Say: "It'll look premium. But their site has 40 people behind it and yours has a phone number that needs to ring. Different jobs."
Why: resets the goal to revenue, not vanity.

**42. "What do you need from me?"**
Say: "Logo, photos if you have them, and 20 minutes for the intake list. Miss the photos and I use pro stock until you swap them. Nothing blocks the build."
Why: shows the machine is real. Handled sells.

## PROCESS, GHOSTING, CLOSING (43-50)

**43. Silence after the proposal (3+ days).**
Say: "No pitch, just closing the loop. If it's a no, tell me and I'll stop. If it's a not-yet, when should I check back?"
Why: the permission-to-say-no message gets more replies than any follow-up trick.

**44. "I need to ask my wife/partner/business partner."**
Say: "Smart. Send them the proposal link, it's built to be read in 2 minutes. Want me on a 10-minute call with both of you so nothing gets lost in translation?"
Why: gets you into the real decision room instead of exiled from it.

**45. "Can you send a contract?"**
Say: "The proposal IS the scope. I'll send the one-page agreement with the deposit link today. You can be a line item on my calendar by Friday."
Why: momentum. Paper fast, build slot named.

**46. "What happens after it launches?"**
Say: "Two options: you own it fully and call me when you need me, or Care Growth at [CARE_GROWTH] a month, I keep it updated, backed up, and edited. Most pick care after the first month of owning it alone."
Why: honest choice, seeded with the observed pattern.

**47. "Remove me / stop contacting me."**
Say: "Done, you won't hear from me again. If anything changes, my name finds easily. Good luck out there."
Why: instant, clean, zero guilt. Also the law. (Concierge: tag NO_GO immediately.)

**48. They rebook the third time.**
Say: "Life happens. Here's my honest read: if this isn't a priority, say so and I'll close the file, no hard feelings. If it is, grab any slot this week and we're done in 15."
Why: walk-away trigger from the pricing tree, delivered kindly.

**49. "Ok... so what do I do now?" (buying signal)**
Say: "Two things. Tap the deposit link, then send me your logo. Build starts today, preview Thursday. Welcome aboard."
Why: never let a yes float. Concrete next steps in one breath.

**50. "Can you guarantee results?"**
Say: "I guarantee the build, the timeline, and that you approve before live. Nobody honest guarantees Google. What I'll show you is the before and after numbers of people who came before you."
Why: refuses the trap, replaces it with the strongest honest thing.
