# LinkedIn Comment Formula (from [OWNER]'s father, veteran copywriter — 2026-07-11)

The 4-part structure for every comment on someone else's post. Wired into the
networking engine (`second-brain/agents/networking.py` COMMENT_PROMPT); use it for
any hand-written comment too.

## The structure
1. **Sincere thank-you or compliment referencing something specific** from the post:
   a phrase, story, or point that stood out. Never bare praise; the specificity is
   what makes it sincere (and what passes the anti-bot filler screen).
2. **Quick personal reaction or observation**, one or two sentences, from real reps.
3. **One short line on why the insight matters** for you or others reading.
4. **End with a curious, open-ended question** the author will want to answer.

## Tone
- Warm, conversational, peer-to-peer.
- Punchy sentences, no fluff. Slightly personal, concise.
- Like a thoughtful remark dropped in a busy LinkedIn thread.
- House rules still apply: no emojis, no hashtags, no em-dashes, exactly one
  question (last), never correct or lecture the poster.

## Reference examples (agency-lane)
> Really liked the line, "The problem isn't the offer. It's the fulfillment." I've
> seen agencies close great clients and then get buried trying to deliver everything
> themselves. That one sentence explains a lot. At what point do you think most
> agency owners realize they need help?

> This hit home. A lot of people spend all their time figuring out how to get more
> clients, then realize delivery becomes the real challenge. Good systems make
> growth a lot less stressful. What's the first thing you'd fix inside an agency
> that's starting to feel overloaded?

> Great post. Breaking it down into scripts, videos, carousels, and editing really
> shows how much work happens after the sale. Clients usually remember the delivery
> more than the pitch. Which part of the fulfillment process do you see agencies
> underestimate the most?

## Anti-template guardrails (keep the batch human)
- Vary openers: "Thanks for sharing the story about...", "Really liked the line...",
  "I appreciated your point about...", "This hit home.", "I liked the way you explained..."
- Vary length 35-70 words across a batch (uniform lengths read templated).
- Skip posts not genuinely worth a real comment; never force one.
