# Local-Biz Objection Playbook — counters in [OWNER]'s voice

_2026-07-07. The existing 50-item bank (objections.md) skews agency and
web-buyer. This one is the LOCAL BUSINESS set: what a medspa, a men's-health
clinic, a contractor, or a salon actually says on the [SECOND_BRAND] re-open
call. Same format as objections.md: what they say, what you say, why it works.
Say the counter, then STOP TALKING. Silence closes. No em-dashes. Word-for-word
usable. Pairs with warm-reopen-call-framework.md and the niche books._

## Rule of engagement
These owners are not shopping a website. They raised a hand about more customers.
So most objections here are really "why do I need this" and "I already have that
handled," not price haggling. Diagnose first (warm-reopen-call-framework.md stage
2), THEN these counters land, because they rest on the number and the leak the
owner already told you. A counter without their number is just an argument.

## "I ALREADY HAVE SOMEONE / SOMETHING" (1-12)

**1. "I already have someone doing my Instagram / social."**
Say: "Keep them, social is finding you customers. My question is where those
customers land. They tap your bio link and hit what, exactly? If it is a page
with no booking, no prices, no faces, you are paying for the click and dropping
the catch."
Why: does not attack the thing they pay for. Moves the fight to the leak, which
is your lane.

**2. "My nephew / cousin / friend built my site."**
Say: "Great, then this doubles as his spec sheet. If he can hit what I am
showing you at this price, hire him. If he is still getting to it in two weeks,
call me."
Why: zero defensiveness. Time and the spec do the work, not gatekeeping. Straight
from the pricing tree.

**3. "I get all my business from word of mouth."**
Say: "That is the best kind, do not lose it. But a new mover or someone with an
emergency at 9pm does not have your number, they have Google. Word of mouth does
not catch the customer who has never heard of you. That is the one your site is
for."
Why: honors what works, names the exact gap word of mouth cannot cover.

**4. "All my clients come from referrals / repeat customers."**
Say: "Then your reputation is already doing the hard part. The site just has to
not lose the person your happy client sends. Right now they get referred, they
Google you, and they land on a page that makes them hesitate. That is a referral
you paid nothing for and still dropped."
Why: reframes the site as protecting existing goodwill, not chasing strangers.

**5. "I already have a marketing agency / I already pay for ads."**
Say: "Keep them for the ads. I fix where the ads land. Ask them what your
booking conversion is. If they cannot answer, that is the problem, and it is not
one more ads will solve. You are buying more water for a leaking bucket."
Why: true, disarming, and it hands them a test their current vendor fails.

**6. "I have Vagaro / Fresha / my booking system already."**
Say: "Keep it, I do not replace your scheduler, I embed it. What I fix is
everything before the booking: the reason they trust you enough to click it.
Right now that link goes to a bare rental page with no faces and no proof."
Why: kills the "you are trying to sell me something I have" fear and re-aims at
the real gap.

**7. "My EHR / telehealth platform already has a booking page." (clinic)**
Say: "Perfect, we use it. I do not touch your scheduler. I build the part that
gets a patient to trust you enough to book: the provider, the credentials, the
program, the price. The scheduler takes the booking, it does not earn it."
Why: separates the tool they have from the job it does not do.

**8. "I already tried marketing and it did not work."**
Say: "What did they actually deliver that you could count? Most of these gigs
ship a monthly report and zero new customers. I do not do retainers and reports.
Fixed price, one countable outcome, and you approve the work before it goes
live."
Why: names the burn most of them carry, then positions as the opposite of it.

**9. "I have a Facebook page, is that not enough?"**
Say: "Facebook is where they check if you are real. It is not where they book,
compare your prices, or find your services at 9pm. When they are ready to spend
money they Google you, and that is where you need to win. The page is the
handshake, the site is the sale."
Why: assigns each channel its real job, shows the gap without trashing the page.

**10. "I already rank on Google / I am on Google."**
Say: "Being found is step one. Getting the click and the call is step two.
Someone searches, sees you and two others, and picks on trust: reviews, a real
site, a fast load. If your site is slow or thin, you rank and still lose the
call to the guy below you who looks safer."
Why: separates visibility from conversion, the part they are actually losing.

**11. "I have a guy who does my SEO / online stuff."**
Say: "Then ask him one thing: what happens when someone lands on the site he is
ranking? If the page loses them, he is driving traffic to a leak. Keep him, and
let me fix where his traffic goes. We are not competing, we are two halves of the
same job."
Why: non-territorial, and it makes their current spend more effective, not
wasted.

**12. "I use Linktree / a Calendly link, that works fine."**
Say: "It takes a booking, sure. It does not sell anyone on booking. A cold
prospect hits a list of links or a bare calendar with no reason to trust you,
and most of them bounce. You are catching the people already sold and losing
everyone on the fence."
Why: concedes the tool works for warm traffic, exposes what it drops.

## "DO I EVEN NEED THIS" (13-22)

**13. "I am not sure I even need a website."**
Say: "Maybe you do not. Let me ask: when someone hears about you and wants to
check you out or book, where do they go? If the answer is nowhere solid, that is
customers deciding whether to trust you with nothing to look at. What is a new
customer worth to you?"
Why: does not insist. Makes them picture the moment a site is missing, then
anchors on their number.

**14. "People do not really use websites anymore, it is all social."**
Say: "They use social to discover you and a site to decide. Nobody hands over a
credit card or books an appointment off a scroll. When they are ready to spend,
they Google you. If there is nothing there, or it is bad, that is the moment you
lose them, right when they were ready to buy."
Why: corrects the premise, pins the loss to the highest-intent moment.

**15. "I am already busy / booked solid, I do not need more."**
Say: "Booked with your best-margin work, or whatever comes in the door? A site
that ranks and converts fills the high-value calendar, not just the busy one.
And busy today does not mean busy in your slow season. You build the machine now
so the slow months are not slow."
Why: separates busy from profitable, and flips seasonality into a reason to act.

**16. "My business is doing fine without one."**
Say: "Then this is not about survival, it is about the ceiling. You are doing
fine on the customers who already find you. The question is how many are looking
for exactly what you do and landing on someone else because you were harder to
find or trust. What is that worth over a year?"
Why: reframes from need to opportunity cost, anchors on a number.

**17. "I am too old-school for all this internet stuff."**
Say: "Fair, and you do not have to touch any of it. That is my job. You keep
doing the work you are good at, I handle the part that makes the phone ring. You
give me 20 minutes twice and I run the rest."
Why: removes the effort objection, respects the identity instead of arguing it.

**18. "I am planning to retire / sell in a few years."**
Say: "Then a site that fills the calendar and shows real reviews is exactly what
makes the business worth more when you sell. A buyer pays for a steady stream of
customers, not a phone that only rings from your personal contacts. This is an
asset, not just an expense."
Why: reframes the spend as increasing exit value, meets them where they are
headed.

**19. "I do not have time to deal with building a website."**
Say: "You deal with it twice: 20 minutes to hand me what you have, 20 minutes on
day 3 to approve. I run everything in between and it is live in 7 days. That is
the whole ask on your time."
Why: quantifies THEIR time to almost nothing. Busy people buy handled. From the
50-item bank, adapted.

**20. "Let me get through my busy season first."**
Say: "Your busy season traffic is exactly what the new site should be catching.
Every week it waits is your best-traffic week wasted. Build takes 7 days, start
now and it is live while the traffic is still high."
Why: their calendar argues for you, not against.

**21. "It is my slow season, money is tight right now."**
Say: "Slow season is exactly when the phone needs to ring more, not less. This
is when you build so the busy season pays for it. Come out of the slow months
with it live instead of starting from zero when everyone else does."
Why: seasonality flipped into urgency. Bank counter 12, localized.

**22. "I will get to it eventually / it is on my list."**
Say: "It has been on the list a while, that is why we are talking. Here is the
honest read: if it is not a priority, tell me and I will close the file, no hard
feelings. If it is, we start today and it is done in a week. Which one?"
Why: names the pattern kindly, forces a real yes or no instead of another
someday.

## PRICE, LOCALIZED (23-30)

**23. "That is too expensive."**
Say: "Compared to what? One missed job a month costs you more than this site
does. What is an average job worth for you?"
Why: makes them price the loss, not the site. Their number sells it. Bank
counter 1.

**24. "That is a lot for a website."**
Say: "It is not for a website, it is for the customers it brings in. You told me
a {customer/patient/job} is worth {their number}. This pays for itself in {n} of
them. After that it is profit every month it is up."
Why: moves the frame from cost of the thing to return from the thing, using
their own anchor.

**25. "The place down the street charged way less." / "I got a cheaper quote."**
Say: "Take it if you trust it. Ask them to walk you through five specific things
costing you customers on your current site first. That is the difference you are
paying for. Cheap gets you a template that cannot see your business."
Why: confidence plus a test the cheap option fails. Bank 6, localized.

**26. "Can you do it cheaper?"**
Say: "The price is the price. What I can do is trade: knock 10 percent off for a
review on delivery and two intros to other owners like you. Deal?"
Why: a discount must buy something. Never for silence. Bank 4.

**27. "I just spent my budget on ads / a new sign / equipment."**
Say: "Then you are paying to send people to a site that leaks them. Fix the
bucket before you buy more water. The ads you already paid for work better the
day the site converts."
Why: their existing spend becomes the argument for this one. Bank 10.

**28. "I need to think about the money."**
Say: "Sure. While you think, the price holds 14 days, then it is a requote. What
number would make this a yes today?"
Why: deadline plus a direct ask that surfaces the real objection. Bank 9.

**29. "Can we do a payment plan?"**
Say: "50 percent to start, 50 on delivery, and delivery is a week out. That is
the payment plan, and half of it comes due only after you have seen it live."
Why: reframes existing terms as the accommodation. Bank 8.

**30. "What if it does not bring me more business?"**
Say: "I guarantee the build, the timeline, and that you approve it before it goes
live. Nobody honest guarantees Google. What I will show you is the before and
after of owners who came before you, and you never pay the balance until you have
seen the real thing on day 3."
Why: refuses the impossible guarantee, replaces it with the strongest honest
thing. Bank 50, localized.

## TRUST, PROCESS, GHOSTING (31-40)

**31. "How do I know you are legit?"**
Say: "You do not yet, that is why you approve a working preview on day 3 before
you pay the balance. And I ran ops for an agency and took a client from 400K to a
million a year. I would rather show you than tell you."
Why: process safety plus the one number that matters. Bank 21.

**32. "Do you have examples / other clients like me?"**
Say: "Yes, I will text you live sites and a case study. While you look, tell me
this: what would YOUR site need to do for this to be a clear win?"
Why: gives proof and immediately re-engages on their goal. Bank 22.

**33. "You are not local, are you even nearby?"**
Say: "Your customers find you on Google, not by driving past my office. I build
for how they actually search and book. And you can call me directly any time,
that part is very local."
Why: relocates local to what matters. Bank 23.

**34. "I got burned by a marketing guy before."**
Say: "Then you know exactly what to watch for. Day 3 preview, approve before it
goes live, everything in writing, half the money only after you have seen it.
Hold me to all of it."
Why: welcomes the scrutiny instead of dodging the wound. Bank 27.

**35. "Are you AI? Did a robot write this?"**
Say: "I use AI in my delivery, that is why I am fast and the price is what it is.
The judgment and the guarantee are me. You are talking to [OWNER]."
Why: honest, turns the tool into the advantage. Never lie about this. Bank 25.

**36. "Websites for my industry are a compliance headache." (clinic / med spa)**
Say: "That is exactly why you want someone who knows the lines. No drug brand
names, no outcome claims, no patient data through the form. I build these clean
and flag anything gray for your medical director. A template shop is where the
trouble comes from."
Why: turns the risk into the reason to pick a specialist over a generalist.

**37. "I need to talk to my partner / spouse / business partner."**
Say: "Smart. Send them the proposal, it is built to read in two minutes. Want me
on a quick call with both of you so nothing gets lost in the retelling?"
Why: gets you into the real decision room instead of exiled from it. Bank 44.

**38. "Send me some info and I will look it over later."**
Say: "Already did, that is the proposal link, a two-minute read. Open it now
while I am on the line and tell me what is wrong with it. Later is where these
die."
Why: compress to now. Later is where deals go to rot. Bank 20.

**39. Silence after the call or proposal (3+ days).**
Say: "No pitch, just closing the loop. If it is a no, tell me and I will stop. If
it is a not-yet, when should I check back?"
Why: permission to say no gets more replies than any follow-up trick. Bank 43.

**40. They reschedule or ghost a third time.**
Say: "Life happens. Honest read: if this is not a priority right now, say so and
I will close the file, no hard feelings. If it is, grab any slot this week and we
are done in 15 minutes."
Why: the walk-away trigger, delivered kindly. Bank 48. Third reschedule is a real
signal, not a hurdle.

## THE REPEAT-OBJECTION RULE
If they raise the SAME objection twice after a clean counter, it is not the real
objection or it is a no. Do not counter a third time. Say: "Sounds like the
timing is not right, and that is fine. The proposal holds 14 days if anything
changes." Then stop. Stacking counters past two makes you the pushy marketer
they were afraid of. One counter, silence, and if it comes back a second time,
one more, then let it go. Log what they said, verbatim, so the bank learns.
