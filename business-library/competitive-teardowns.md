they honestly win, and where they fail. The point: ground the wedge in reality so
pitches contrast against what buyers actually see, not a strawman. Companion:

## 1. Fiverr and marketplace freelancers

**What they actually offer.** A gig catalog. Template-based WordPress/Wix/Shopify
builds, tiered packages (basic/standard/premium), portfolio thumbnails, buyer
reviews, escrow through the platform. Delivery windows commonly 2-7 days on
template work. MARKET-TYPICAL pricing: $100-600 for a template build with light
customization; [LANDING_PAGE]-2,500+ for top-rated sellers doing semi-custom work.

**Where they genuinely win. Say this out loud when true.**
- **Price.** Nothing beats [REFERRAL_FEE] when [REFERRAL_FEE] is the budget. Our own counter concedes
  it: "You can. You'll get a template with your name typed into it"
  (`playbooks/objections.md` #2). "[REFERRAL_FEE] buys a look. It doesn't buy a diagnosis"
  (`content/objection-column/03-fiverr-200.md`).
- **Volume and choice.** Thousands of sellers, instant start, reviews as a rough
  quality signal, escrow if it goes sideways.
- **Fine for throwaway jobs.** A placeholder page, a one-off graphic, a test. If
  price is the only column that matters, they win, and that's ok (objections #34).

**Where they fail for our buyers.**
- **No diagnosis.** The gig starts from a template, not from what's costing the
  business customers. Templates can't see (objection column #3). Our build starts
  with the teardown; that's the moat (objections #33).
- **The agency still does the project management.** Specs, revisions, chasing,
  QA. The agency owner saves cash and spends the thing they're actually out of:
  attention. The hidden cost never shows on the gig price.
- **Quality variance and anonymity.** Ratings smooth over the tail risk, and the
  tail risk lands in front of the agency's client. The audit scored Fiverr 2/10 on
  differentiation and 1/10 on agency-business understanding
  (`~/Claude/MARKETING-AUDIT-linkedin.md`).
- **No invisibility discipline.** White-label is a settings checkbox to a gig
  seller, not a policy. There's no NDA-standard, no confidentiality doc, no stake
  in the agency's client relationship.
- **Revision economics.** Cheap gigs monetize revisions. The [REFERRAL_FEE] site becomes
  $500 across three change orders, delivered slowly.

**When we lose anyway.** A buyer who only needs "a look," has time to manage the
seller, and carries no client-relationship risk. Let them go kindly.

## 2. Offshore dev shops and contractors

**What they actually offer.** Hourly or fixed-bid development, mostly WordPress/
PHP/Shopify, often through Upwork or a small shop relationship. Real capacity,
real skills at the top end. MARKET-TYPICAL pricing: $5-25/hr; [CARE_PREMIUM]-1,500 for a
full small-business site; retainer arrangements for ongoing capacity.

**Where they genuinely win.**
- **Raw cost per hour.** Unbeatable. At scale, with strong internal PM and written
  specs, offshore capacity is how big shops protect margin.
- **Capacity.** A team can parallelize what one operator can't. Our solo capacity
  is real and finite: roughly 10 builds + 2 installs + care per month at the
  $25k/mo run-rate (`operating-model.md`, capacity truth).
- **A good one, well-managed, is a fine answer.** If the agency has a dedicated PM
  and a spec library, we don't pretend they're wrong.

**Where they fail for our buyers.**
- **They need something to be handed.** This is the documented one. The
  [PRIOR_BASELINE]->$1M client had a growing roster of overseas freelancers "making things
  worse, not better, because there was nothing to hand them"
  (`~/Claude/second-brain/store/case/400k-to-1m.html`). No SOPs, no system, no
  output. [OWNER] didn't read that in a blog post; he fixed it from the COO chair.
- **The management tax.** Timezone lag turns every revision loop into a lost day.
  The ICP's stated pain is "babysitting unreliable offshore contractors"
  (`icp-and-personas.md`). The owner becomes the PM, which is the exact job they
  were trying to buy their way out of.
- **Quality drift and turnover.** The good developer gets promoted off your
  account. The agency finds out mid-build.
- **No client context.** They build what the ticket says. They don't know the
  client is one missed deadline from leaving, because nobody told them and they
  wouldn't price it in anyway. Audit score: 1/10 agency-business understanding.

**When we lose anyway.** High-volume agencies with real internal PM and 10+ builds
a month. Honest answer: we're not their vendor, we're their overflow lane, and the
Bench Retainer idea exists for exactly that seam (offers.md, TODO-confirm status).

## 3. Productized white-label agencies (DashClicks / Vendasta / UnlimitedWP style)

**What they actually offer.** Subscription or SKU'd fulfillment with a brand:
white-label dashboards, published SLAs, teams with redundancy, integrations,
sales collateral the agency can reuse. MARKET-TYPICAL pricing: $500-3,000 per
site depending on tier; subscription dev plans commonly $500-2,000/mo. The audit
pegs the premium productized tier at $2.5-8K per build
(`~/Claude/MARKETING-AUDIT-linkedin.md`).

**Where they genuinely win.**
- **Published guarantees and SLAs.** They scored 7/10 on risk-reversal in the
  audit, our weakest visible row (3/10, because unlimited revisions existed but
  wasn't surfaced). Fix is in flight: surface the guarantee everywhere.
- **Team redundancy.** Nobody's build stalls because one person got sick. Against
  a solo operator, that's a real objection, not FUD. Our honest counter is the
  system (documented method, scripted pipeline, contractor bench planned at the
  hiring trigger, `operating-model.md`), not a claim that it can't happen.
- **Brand trust at scale.** Case-study libraries, branded testimonials, years of
  marketing surface. 7/10 on trust vs our 5/10.

**Where they fail for our buyers.**
- **Founder-absent process.** The audit line: "process-fluent, founder-absent"
  (5/10 agency understanding). The agency talks to an account coordinator reading
  a runbook. Judgment calls wait in a queue.
- **Price.** At $2.5-8K they cost what the agency wants to CHARGE, which breaks
  the resell math. Our [FIRST_BUILD]-1,200 against their resale of [ECOM_PRICE]-5,000 is the
  margin story (`~/Claude/MONEY-THIS-MONTH.md` §4).
- **Templated soul.** Productized means the 500th site looks like the 400th.
  Passable for citations-and-hosting bundles, weak for a medspa selling trust.
- **No ops upsell.** They will never sit in the agency's P&L and fix the delivery
  pipeline itself. The COO ladder is a lane they structurally can't enter.

**When we lose anyway.** An agency that needs 20 sites a month with contractual
SLAs and vendor-of-record paperwork. Today that's not our lane, and saying so
fast preserves the referral.

## 4. The US white-label freelancer (the nearest substitute)

**What they actually offer.** Same pitch as ours minus the operator story: a solo
US dev doing white-label builds. MARKET-TYPICAL pricing near ours; typical
turnaround 1-2 weeks (audit table: speed 4/10). Timezone and language are soft
edges, not moats.

**Where they win.** Availability and familiarity. Plenty exist; some are good.

**Where they fail.** No structural edge (audit: 5/10 differentiation, "US/timezone
is a soft edge"). Reliability is a personality trait, not a system: no intake
gate, no scripted QA, no published method, no delivery artifacts. And 3/10 on
agency-business understanding: they know delivery, not the agency P&L.

**Our head-to-head.** The [OWNER] Method is the difference made visible
(`[OWNER_HANDLE]-method.md`): the intake gate that makes 48-72hr honest, the QA report
attached to every delivery ("a differentiator most $500 vendors never show,"
`sops/deliver-a-site.md` Stage 7), the receipt in live copy: handed off Monday,
client-ready Thursday, approved on the first round (VOICE-SPEC).

## Honest limits of the wedge (internal candor)
- The wedge wins when the buyer carries client-relationship risk or is drowning in
  delivery. It does nothing for a price-only buyer or a genuinely well-staffed
  shop. Qualify fast, walk kindly (pricing-tree walk-away triggers).
- The COO story is currently proven by one written case study plus his published
  claims; the second-agency $2M+ claim has no written case behind it yet
  (`proof-receipts-library.md` R7). Depth of proof is the wedge's soft spot; keep
  collecting receipts.
- Solo capacity is a real ceiling (~10 builds/mo, `operating-model.md`). Don't
  out-pitch what delivery can hold; the method's credibility IS the product.
