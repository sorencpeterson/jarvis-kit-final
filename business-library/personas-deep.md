# Personas, Deep
_Authored 2026-07-07. Expands the minimal ICP A/B in `icp-and-personas.md` into
full working personas. Every persona here maps to real people in the pipeline: the
58 booked calls and 423 repliers in `~/Claude/WARM-HITLIST.md`, the agency targets
of the white-label campaigns, and the founders in [OWNER]'s own network. Nothing
below is invented demographics; where a detail is inference rather than an observed
fact, it's labeled as a read. Use with `positioning-master.md` (the spine),
`playbooks/objections.md` (the counters), and `playbooks/sales-calls.md` (the call
structure)._

Format per persona: who they are, what they want, what they fear, the buyer
journey, who else is in the room, the line that lands, and what they buy first.

## P1. The Margin-Squeezed Agency Owner ([OWNER_COMPANY] core)

**Who.** Owner of a 3-20 person digital/marketing agency that sells websites but
never staffed real dev (`icp-and-personas.md`). They've sold work they can't
build. Right now they're either doing the builds themselves at night or babysitting
an offshore contractor who needs more management than the client does.

**What they want.** A dependable white-label partner they can mark up and resell.
They resell at [ECOM_PRICE]-5,000 against [OWNER]'s [FIRST_BUILD]-1,200 cost
(`~/Claude/MONEY-THIS-MONTH.md` §4), so the math sells itself once trust exists.
They're buying reliability, not creativity (`playbooks/sales-calls.md` ICP B).

**What they fear.** Being embarrassed in front of their client. A vendor who's late,
who ships sloppy work they have to fix at midnight, or worst of all, who goes
around them to the client. The whole purchase is a trust decision wearing a price
tag. Their last vendor's failure is the spec for winning them: ask "what did your
last vendor get wrong?" and build the pitch on the answer (sales-calls, Stage 2).

**Buyer journey.** Referral or a LinkedIn/cold touch -> they look [OWNER] up
(the profile's job is to not lose them, per `~/Claude/MARKETING-AUDIT-linkedin.md`)
-> scope conversation -> [FIRST_BUILD] flat test build with real stakes -> rate card ->
3+ orders/mo earns partner pricing, 10% off, floors $700/[FIRST_BUILD] (pricing-tree).
Proof point that this journey works: Acme Co Soft closed [STANDARD_SITE] off the white-label
email sequence without ever opening the proposal link; the deal closed in the
conversation itself (`proof-receipts-library.md` R2). Decision speed: days, when
they have a client waiting.

**Who else is in the room.** Their client, invisibly; every promise [OWNER] makes,
the agency re-makes to someone else, marked up. Sometimes a partner or an account
manager. And the ghost of the last vendor. There is no committee; the owner signs.

**The line that lands.** "You sell the site, I build it, your name stays on it"
(sales-calls ICP B open). Backed by the identity line: "I'm the call you make when
your pipeline outruns your dev capacity and a client needs something shipped this
week" (VOICE-SPEC, live copy).

**First purchase.** Agency first build, [FIRST_BUILD] flat. Then the rate card. The
upsell path nobody else offers: once they trust the builds, the COO ladder (ops
audit -> Ops Partner Lite) is sitting right there, because their delivery
bottleneck is rarely only websites.

**Register.** Operator to operator. Pipeline, fulfillment, margin, markup. Never
marketer-speak. They read emojis as amateur hour (VOICE-SPEC).

## P2. The Owner-Injector (medspa, beachhead niche)

**Who.** NP/RN/MD who owns the medspa and does the injecting
(`sops/niche-books/medspa.md`, "who signs the check"). Clinical-first, suspicious
of marketers, allergic to jargon. Most were burned by a $1,500-2,500/mo retainer
agency that produced reports, not bookings (medspa.md §3). The warm list is
majority this persona.

**What they want.** The calendar full of high-margin patients: tox, filler, laser
packages, memberships, and lately the GLP-1 weight-loss line ([CARE_PREMIUM]-500/mo
memberships, the fastest-growing revenue line per medspa.md §4). They understand
patient LTV cold: a retained aesthetics patient is worth $1,500-5,000/yr
(medspa.md §1). Talk patient volume and no-show rates, not CTR.

**What they fear.** Wasting money on marketing again. Compliance exposure
(before/afters without consent, outcome claims). Looking cheap to a clientele that
buys trust. And quietly: that their Fresha/Vagaro rental page with 4 photos is
costing them patients they never see, which our 9 teardowns confirmed is exactly
what's happening (medspa.md §2).

**Buyer journey.** They raised a hand to a [SECOND_BRAND] campaign months ago
about getting more patients (that's how they're on the warm list) -> re-open call,
vague, no pitch -> diagnosis on the phone -> teardown with their real faults ->
proposal at [ECOM_PRICE] booking tier minimum, White-Glove [WHITE_GLOVE] if the site is dead
(medspa.md pricing defaults). Decision cycles are fast, days not months, when the
pain is bookings; slow when it's "brand" (medspa.md). So sell bookings.

**Who else is in the room.** The practice manager (P3, often the real driver). The
incumbent ads agency, whom we don't attack: "keep them for ads, I fix where the
ads land" (medspa.md objection 2). A spouse or business partner on bigger tickets
(objections #44). And compliance anxiety, which sits in every conversation as a
silent stakeholder; naming the consent-gated gallery and claims-clean copy builds
more trust than any portfolio (medspa.md compliance rails).

**The line that lands.** "Instagram finds them, your site loses them. They tap
your bio link and land on a page with no faces, no prices, no proof. You're paying
for the click and dropping the catch" (medspa.md objection 1). Close the money
logic with: "One retained tox patient is about [PROJECT_EXAMPLE] a year. Two patients pay for
the whole site" (medspa.md ROI anchor).

**First purchase.** E-com/Booking [ECOM_PRICE] or White-Glove [WHITE_GLOVE]. Care attach at
delivery, Growth+ [CARE_PREMIUM]/mo pitched as the default because seasonal content is
genuinely valuable here, Growth [CARE_GROWTH] as the floor (medspa.md; [CARE_PREMIUM] tier is
PROPOSED-v2 in offers.md, confirm before quoting).

**Register.** Plain clinical respect. Zero hype. Reference their booking platform
by name (Vagaro, Fresha, Boulevard, Jane) and never propose replacing it, embed it
(medspa.md build spec 1).

## P3. The Practice Manager (medspa / clinic, the other signature)

**Who.** Runs the front of house for an owner-injector or a small clinic group.
Owns the phones, the schedule, the no-show problem. Often the one who booked the
original No BS call.

**What they want.** The phone ringing and the calendar filling (medspa.md, "who
signs the check"). Fewer manual steps: they live the cost of a booking flow that
requires them to call people back.

**What they fear.** Bringing the owner something that flops. They're spending
someone else's money and their own credibility. They also fear tools that add
work: a new system they have to babysit.

**Buyer journey.** Same warm-list origin as P2, but they evaluate operationally:
show, don't pitch. The day-3 preview is built for them; they can click the booking
flow themselves before anything goes live (pricing-tree objection pre-empts). They
recommend; the owner signs. Arm them to sell it internally: one page, the faults
found, the price, the timeline.

**Who else is in the room.** The owner (final yes), front desk staff who'll field
whatever the site produces, sometimes the booking-platform rep.

**The line that lands.** "Booking above the fold, because the 9pm impulse booker
doesn't call back tomorrow" (medspa.md build spec 1: the site's job is to catch
the after-hours decision they currently lose).

**First purchase.** Same tiers as P2. The practice manager is also the natural
care-plan champion: monthly edits and seasonal pushes are their workload we're
absorbing (Care Growth/Growth+).

**Register.** Concrete and operational. What changes Monday morning, who does
what, what they'll never have to touch.

## P4. The Men's-Health Clinic Operator (TRT / wellness, warm-list heavy)

**Who.** Owner or operator of a men's TRT/wellness clinic. The 58 booked calls are
thick with them: Riverbend Men's Health (three separate locations), Rockville Men's
Health, Summit Male Medical, Optimal T, Elevate T, Texas Low-T, Tier 1 Health
(`~/Claude/WARM-HITLIST.md`). Some are franchise operators, which changes the
room (see below). Read: this niche books consults like medspas book tox, so the
clinic playbook applies with a discretion overlay.

**What they want.** Booked consults and membership revenue. TRT is a
subscription business: the patient who starts therapy stays for years, so one
converted consult carries serious LTV. (Membership pricing for TRT is
market-typical [CARE_GROWTH]-400/mo; UNVERIFIED in this library, confirm before using in
copy.) Many are adding medical weight loss; the GLP-1 economics from the medspa
book carry over directly ([CARE_PREMIUM]-500/mo memberships, medspa.md §4).

**What they fear.** Wasted ad spend, same as medspa. Plus a discretion problem the
medspa doesn't have: their patient doesn't want to explain low T to a receptionist.
Compliance too: testosterone and weight-loss copy has the same claims and
brand-name landmines as GLP-1 (medspa.md compliance rails apply).

**Buyer journey.** Warm-list re-open call (they booked with No BS months ago and
nobody called back) -> "still looking to get more patients booked?" -> teardown ->
booking-tier proposal. Franchise wrinkle: a Riverbend-style operator has corporate
brand standards in the room, so the pitch is location pages and local booking
inside their brand rules, not a rebrand. Multi-location pricing exists as a
PROPOSED-v2 SKU ($850-950/location, offers.md, confirm before quoting).

**Who else is in the room.** Franchise corporate (brand standards, sometimes an
approved-vendor process). A medical director whose license is on the wall. The
practice manager running the phones.

**The line that lands.** Read, built from the observed pattern: "Your patient
decides at 11pm and won't call a front desk about low T. If he can't book a
consult quietly online, he books the clinic that lets him."

**First purchase.** E-com/Booking [ECOM_PRICE] default (booking IS the business,
pricing-tree routing). Webfix [WEBFIX] when the site is salvageable and the wallet is
tight. Care attach at delivery.

**Register.** Straight talk, patient volume and consults booked. Discretion
acknowledged, never dramatized.

## P5. The Buried Solo Founder (the ops ladder buyer)

**Who.** Agency owner or solo founder who IS the business, around the case-study
client's size (roughly [PRIOR_BASELINE] a year when [OWNER] stepped in). That client was
exactly this persona: single owner, one or two employees,
a roster of overseas freelancers making things worse because there was nothing to
hand them, tasks living in his head, an account churning every 2-3 months
(`~/Claude/second-brain/store/case/400k-to-1m.html`).

**What they want.** Out of the middle of every task. Retention that doesn't depend
on their personal heroics. A business that runs when they stop pushing.

**What they fear.** That stepping back means quality collapses, because last time
it did. Consultants with binders. Paying for advice when what they need is
installation. And the quiet one: that the business is worthless without them.

**Buyer journey.** They know [OWNER] or arrive by referral (Andrew's network, the
law firm thread, own network; warm channels are where these deals actually move,
`icp-and-personas.md`) -> the honest question: "are you still buried in delivery,
or have you been able to pull back?" -> $1,500 Ops Audit as the small first yes,
"worst case you get the full plan and run it yourself"
(`~/Claude/MONEY-THIS-MONTH.md` §5) -> Ops Partner Lite [OPS_RETAINER]/mo, one system
installed per month -> AI Ops Install [AI_OPS_PRICE] when they run on GHL-like ops
(offers.md). Slow lane by design: bigger ticket, higher trust, runs behind the
build business (`~/Claude/DOLLARS-ASAP-plan.md`).

**Who else is in the room.** A spouse or partner who's watched the burnout. The
small team who'll live inside whatever gets installed. Sometimes their biggest
client, whose churn risk is the real deadline.

**The line that lands.** "The fix is not another hire. It is the system that makes
the hire work" (case study). And the close of that same page: "build what the
founder cannot be."

**First purchase.** $1,500 Ops Audit. The proof: the case study results, stated
plainly. Revenue [PRIOR_BASELINE] to over $1M a year, churn from every 2-3 months to 2+ year
retention, deliverable quality up roughly 70%, founder out of daily operations
(case study; usage rules in `proof-receipts-library.md` R1).

**Register.** Peer who's carried the same weight. No productivity-guru tone. The
case study does the talking.

## Cross-persona rules
1. **Warm beats cold for every persona** (`icp-and-personas.md`). The 481 already
   raised a hand; call before campaigning.
2. **Re-open vague on the warm list.** They asked No BS for growth, not a website.
   Diagnose on the phone, then quote what fits (`~/Claude/MONEY-THIS-MONTH.md`).
3. **Price in their unit, not ours.** Jobs for trades, patients for clinics, margin
   for agencies, hours-of-their-life for founders (sales-calls).
4. **The objection bank covers all five personas** (`playbooks/objections.md`, 50
   counters). Say the counter, then stop talking.
5. **Every persona's proof needs live in one place:** `proof-receipts-library.md`.
   Don't restate numbers from memory.
