## Why this niche wins for us
1. **Booking is not a feature. It is the business.** A salon that cannot take an
   online appointment is losing revenue every hour it is closed. The pricing tree
   routes salon straight to Booking [ECOM_PRICE] for exactly this reason. The value is
   obvious to the owner, which makes it an easy sell.
2. **No-shows are a bleeding wound they feel personally.** An HVAC no-show is an
   annoyance. A salon no-show is an empty chair for a slot that can never be sold
   again. That chair had a stylist standing at it. No-show recovery (reminders,
   confirmations, deposit-to-book, waitlist backfill) is the single sharpest
   value prop in this book. Lead with it.
3. **Visual business, weak sites.** They sell a look and a vibe, then run a bare
   Fresha or Vagaro rental page with no photos, no team, no personality. The gap
   between the aesthetic they sell and the site they show is the whole pitch,
   same as medspa.
4. **Recurring clients and rebooking.** A good salon client comes back every four
   to six weeks. The site's job is to make the first booking effortless and the
   rebooking automatic. Owners understand client lifetime value once you frame it
   as "a color client is worth $X a year, how many do you lose to a clunky
   booking flow."

## Who signs the check
The owner, almost always. Sometimes an owner-stylist still behind the chair,
sometimes a manager running the front desk and the books. The owner-stylist is
time-starved and hates being sold to. The manager cares about the calendar
filling and the front-desk phone not ringing off the hook for bookings that
should self-serve. Talk in appointments and chair-time, not dollars and
impressions. Decision cycles are fast when the pain is no-shows or a broken
booking flow.

## What their site must do (our build spec, in priority order)
1. **Book above the fold, on their existing system.** Embed what they run
   (Vagaro, Fresha, Boulevard, GlossGenius, Square, Booksy, Mindbody). Never
   propose replacing the booking system, wire it in. The client browsing
   Instagram at 9pm books now or forgets by morning.
2. **The work, shown.** A real gallery of their actual results, not stock. This
   is a visual purchase. Before-and-afters for color and transformations, a
   portfolio grid for the rest.
3. **The team, with faces.** Clients pick a stylist, not just a salon. Photos,
   names, specialties, and a way to book that specific person.
4. **A service menu with price anchors.** "Color from $X, cut from $Y." Hiding
   prices sends the client to the salon down the street that shows them.
5. **No-show defenses wired in.** Confirmation and reminder flow, and where the
   booking system supports it, a deposit or card-on-file to hold the slot. This
   is the feature that pays for the whole build. Make it visible in the pitch.
6. **Reviews streamed in** (Google especially, salons live on local reviews).
7. **Mobile-first, hard.** This audience books from a phone off Instagram at
   night. Thumb-reachable booking or nothing.

## The no-show recovery wedge (lead with this)
This is the sharpest, most quantifiable pain in the niche. Work it on the call.
- **Make them do the math out loud.** "How many no-shows a week? What is an
  average service worth?" Five no-shows a week at an $80 service is $400 a week,
  north of $20,000 a year, in chairs that can never be resold. That number, from
  their mouth, sells the whole build.
- **The fix is concrete, not vague.** Automated confirmations cut no-shows.
  Reminder texts cut them more. A deposit or card-on-file to book cuts them hard.
  Waitlist backfill turns a cancellation into a filled slot instead of a hole.
- **Frame the site as the front door to that machine.** "The site is where they
  book. The reminders and the deposit are what make them actually show. I wire
  both into what you already run."
- Real average-service-value and typical no-show rate per salon: [NEEDS [OWNER]'S
  DATA]. Anchor to their number on the call, not a made-up average.

## Seasonality calendar (drives outreach + care-plan content)
- **Nov-Dec: holiday and event season.** Peak booking, peak no-show pressure,
  gift-card sales. A gift-card page pays for itself here. Highest-demand window.
- **Spring (Mar-May): prom, wedding, and event season.** Booking volume spikes,
  packages and trials move, no-show rates climb with the volume.
- **Summer:** steadier, good window to build so fall and holiday traffic hits a
  finished site.
- **Back-to-school (Aug-Sep):** a real bump for cuts and color resets.

## Their objections (expected) and our counters
1. "I already have Vagaro / Fresha, why do I need a website?" -> "Vagaro takes
   the booking. It does not sell the client on booking. They land on a rental
   page with no faces, no gallery, no reason to pick you over the salon next
   door. The site is the reason they book. Vagaro is just the calendar behind
   it."
2. "All my clients come from Instagram." -> "Instagram finds them. Your link in
   bio loses them. They tap it and hit a booking page with no photos and no
   prices, and half of them bounce. You are paying for the follower and dropping
   the booking."
3. "My no-shows are just part of the business." -> "How many a week? What is a
   service worth? That is real money in chairs you can never resell. I wire in
   reminders and a deposit to book. You do not have to eat that anymore."
4. "I do not have time to deal with a website." -> "You deal with it twice: 20
   minutes to hand me photos and the menu, 20 minutes on day 3 to approve. I run
   the rest. Booking goes up, front-desk calls go down. That is the trade."
5. Price -> anchor to the no-show number and the lifetime value of a color
   client, then the payback in appointments.

## The salon call script (the piece the skeleton was missing)
Salon owners are close to ICP A in tone (owner-operator, wants a direct pitch),
but the anchor is appointments and no-shows, not "average job." Use this frame.

**Stage 1, Open (30 sec):** "Hey {name}, [OWNER]. You booked a call about the
salon a while back and we never connected, that one is on me. I will keep this to
15 minutes. Before I talk: what made you reach out?"
Rule: their reason first. It steers the call.

**Stage 2, Dig (5 min):** three questions, in order:
1. "What is an average appointment worth to you, and what is a regular color or
   cut client worth over a year?" (sets the anchor)
2. "How many no-shows do you eat in a normal week?" (the wound, quantified)
3. "Where do new clients find you now, and what happens when they hit your
   booking page?"
Rule: their numbers, out of their mouth, said back to them.

**Stage 3, Show (3 min):** walk one fault from their teardown. "Your booking
link goes to a Fresha page with no photos and no prices. A client at 9pm off your
Instagram sees that and books the salon that showed her the work. You paid for
that follower."
Rule: one fault fully felt beats five listed.

**Stage 4, Price (2 min):** "Booking build is [ECOM_PRICE]. Half now, half when it is
live in 7 days, you approve the preview on day 3. It wires your booking, your
gallery, and the reminder and deposit flow that kills your no-shows. Based on the
{no-show number} you just gave me, it pays for itself in a few weeks of chairs
you stop losing." Then silence. Count to five.

**Stage 5, Close:** "Want it handled?" Yes: deposit link and logo today. Maybe:
name the objection, use the counter above, ask once more, then set a 48-hour
deadline.
