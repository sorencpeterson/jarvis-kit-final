## Why this niche wins for us
1. **Repeatable, no re-selling.** A direct client is one build then a referral
   ask. A happy agency partner sends order after order without a new pitch each
   time. One partner clearing three builds a month is worth more than ten
   one-off local jobs, at a fraction of the sales effort.
2. **They already sold the site. The pressure is on THEIR side.** By the time an
   agency calls me, they have a signed client waiting. They are not shopping,
   they are unblocking. That is the easiest buyer in the world: motivated,
   deadlined, and done haggling.
3. **The bottleneck is real and it is everywhere.** Agencies sell websites as
   part of a stack, then choke on delivery. The owner is doing dev they hate at
   11pm, or babysitting an offshore contractor who missed the deadline again, or
   turning down web work they cannot fulfill. That bottleneck is the entire
   pitch and it never goes away as they grow.
4. **Margin math they can do in their head.** They bill their client $3,000 to
   [AI_OPS_PRICE] for a site. My first build is [FIRST_BUILD] flat. They keep the spread and
   never touch a line of code. Once they feel that spread on one build, the math
   sells every build after.

## Who signs the check
The owner or founder of a small-to-mid agency, roughly 3 to 20 people. Sometimes
a delivery lead or ops manager they trust to pick vendors. This person is an
operator, not a marketer being pitched. They think in pipeline, fulfillment,
margin, markup, and client swirl. They are not buying creativity, they are
buying reliability and invisibility. Their deepest fear is a vendor who
embarrasses them in front of a client or, worse, goes around them to that
client. Address them operator-to-operator. The receipt closes them, not the
adjectives.

## What the ENGAGEMENT must do (our spec, from their side)
This is not a build spec like the medspa book. The product an agency buys is a
delivery experience. Get these right and they come back.
1. **Fast, predictable turnaround.** Handed off Monday, client-ready Thursday.
   48-72 hour working draft on standard scope. The agency's whole reason to use
   me is that I am faster and more certain than their current option. Hit the
   date every time. The date is the product.
2. **A day-3 preview THEY can forward.** They send their client a working link
   under their own brand and look like heroes. I am never on that email. The
   preview is their proof to their client, not mine to them.
3. **White-label everything.** Their brand, their domain, their handoff. No
   [OWNER_COMPANY] anywhere in the deliverable, the files, the footer, or the
   emails. Their client never learns I exist. NDA standard, offered before they
   ask.
4. **Written scope so their client cannot creep on them.** I hand the agency a
   one-page scope they can put their name on. It protects THEIR margin from
   THEIR client's "while you're in there." Two revision rounds, then it is an
   add-on. That boundary is a gift to a margin-squeezed owner.
5. **Revisions until their client signs off, within reason.** On the first build
   especially, I go the distance so their client approves. The first build is a
   trust purchase. I treat it like one.
6. **Zero client relationship on my side.** I do not email their client, do not
   join their calls unless they invite me as their team, do not follow up for a
   testimonial with their client. The relationship is theirs. I stay behind the
   scenes. That invisibility is the product as much as the site is.

## The invisibility rules (say these; they close the trust gap)
The number-one thing standing between me and an agency's order is "will you go
around me." Answer it before they ask.
- **NDA standard, not on request.** I offer it. Signaling I expect to be
  invisible is more convincing than agreeing to be when cornered.
- **Their brand on everything.** Deliverables, staging links, any file they
  might forward. If their client ever sees a [OWNER_COMPANY] logo, I failed.
- **Their client never learns I exist.** No outreach, no branding, no "built by"
  credit, no sneaky footer link. Ever.
- **I take direction from the agency, not the client.** If their client contacts
  me directly, I route it back to the agency. I do not answer over their head.
- **The line that lands: "You look great, I stay out of it."** That is the whole
  model in six words. Say it.

## Why me over their current dev (the wedge)
Every agency already has a way to build sites. I am a replacement, so I need to
beat what they use now. The last-vendor answer is the spec for winning them.
- **vs the offshore contractor / Fiverr:** "I ran ops inside an agency and
  scaled it from [PRIOR_RESULT]. I know what a client breathing down your neck
  feels like. Fiverr cannot say that. Reliability is the whole product and you
  cannot buy it by the hour overseas."
- **vs doing it themselves:** "You did not start an agency to build sites at
  11pm. Hand it off, forward the day-3 preview to your client, get back to
  selling. That is the trade."
- **vs their in-house junior:** "Keep them for the client-facing work. Use me
  for overflow when the pipeline outruns the bench. I am the release valve, not
  the replacement for your team."
- **vs the last white-label vendor who burned them:** ask what that vendor got
  wrong, then answer exactly that. Missed deadlines -> the date is the product,
  day-3 preview proves it early. Went around them -> NDA standard, invisibility
  by design. Bad quality -> revisions until their client signs off on the first
  build. Their wound is my script.

## The wedge questions (discovery, this ICP)
Straight from sales-calls.md ICP B, sharpened.
1. "What is on your bench right now?" (a real order beats a hypothetical)
2. "How many builds a month do you sell?"
3. "What kills your margin today: build time, revisions, or the client swirl?"
4. "What did your last web vendor get wrong?" (the spec for winning them)
5. "When your pipeline outruns your dev capacity, what happens to that work
   today?"

## How this niche is different from the direct-client books
- **Cold works better here than it does for local.** Agencies understand a
  wholesale DM. Local businesses need warm. The cold agency drip is a real
  supplement lane for this niche specifically (see project-agency-cold-outreach
  memory). Warm still beats cold, but this is the one niche where cold is not a
  waste.
- **No compliance rails, but hard invisibility rules.** The medspa and men's-
  health books spend their trust budget on HIPAA and claims. This one spends it
  entirely on NDA and never-touch-the-client.
- **The buyer is deadlined, not shopping.** Match that energy. Move fast, name
  the date, take the order. Do not sell a signed-and-waiting agency owner a
  vision. Unblock them.
- **The offer is a relationship, not a transaction.** Every direct-client close
  ends in a referral ask. Every agency close ends in "send me the next one."
  That is the whole reason this niche funds the business.
