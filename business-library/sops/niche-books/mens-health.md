## Why this niche wins for us
1. **Recurring-revenue patients, and the owner knows it.** GLP-1 programs run
   [REFERRAL_FEE]-500/mo and patients stay months. TRT runs $100-300/mo and patients stay
   years. These owners already think in LTV and monthly retention, so they get
   paying to acquire a patient without you teaching them. One retained patient
   pays for the whole site. Say that.
2. **A gold-rush market with amateur-hour sites.** GLP-1 demand exploded in
   2025-26 and everyone rushed in. Half of them are running a Calendly link and
   a Linktree, or a template that never mentions the program by name. The gap
   between a $400/mo offer and a site that can't take the booking is our pitch.
3. **They live and die on their own Google presence.** These clinics fight
   telehealth giants (Hims, Ro, Found) for the same searches. A weak site means
   they rent patients from a platform that owns the relationship. Owning their
   Google presence and their booking is how they stop being a middleman's lead.
4. **Trust is the entire sale, and trust is buildable on a page.** A man handing
   over a credit card for testosterone or a weekly injection wants to see a real
   provider, real credentials, and a real address. Most of these sites hide all
   three. We put them above the fold. That is conversion, not decoration.

## Who signs the check
Usually a provider-owner (MD, DO, NP, PA) or a clinic operator who runs the
business side for a medical director. The provider is clinical-first and
allergic to marketer-speak: talk patient volume, no-show rates, and consult-to-
start conversion, never CTR or impressions. The operator cares about the phone
ringing and the intake calendar filling. Decision cycles are fast when the pain
is empty consult slots, slower when they frame it as brand. Steer them to the
empty-slots frame.

## What their site must do (our build spec, in priority order)
1. **Book a consult above the fold.** These are consult-first businesses: nobody
   buys TRT off a homepage, they book an intake. Embed what they already run
   (their EHR/telehealth scheduler, Calendly, IntakeQ, whatever) and never
   propose replacing it. The 9pm researcher who wants to start losing weight is
   the whole game. Catch him or the platform does.
2. **The provider, front and center.** Photo, name, license, credentials, and a
   plain line of why-trust-them. Men vet the person before the protocol. A
   faceless clinic reads as a pill mill.
3. **A dedicated page per program.** GLP-1 weight loss gets its own page. TRT
   gets its own page. Peptides, ED, longevity, each its own page with what it is,
   who it is for, and what the first month looks like. A paragraph on a shared
   services page leaves money on the table.
4. **Price anchors and what is included.** "Programs from $X/mo, provider visits
   and labs included" beats hidden pricing. Hiding the number sends the patient
   to the telehealth brand that shows it.
5. **The intake path, made obvious.** Consult, labs, protocol, follow-up. Three
   or four steps. Men want to know what they are signing up for before they
   book.
6. **Reviews streamed in** and financing logos (Cherry, CareCredit) where the
   program price needs them.
7. **Mobile-first, hard.** This audience researches at night on a phone. If the
   consult button is not thumb-reachable, it does not exist.

## Compliance rails (say these out loud, they build trust)
This is the part that separates us from a template shop. Know the lines.
- **No drug brand names in the marketing copy.** Do not write "Ozempic" or
  "Wegovy" or "Mounjaro" on the page. Use "GLP-1 medical weight loss program"
  and "prescription weight loss." Brand names invite trademark and platform-
  policy trouble, especially on anything ad-adjacent. Same discipline as medspa.
- **No outcome or efficacy claims.** "Lose 20 pounds guaranteed" is a
  regulatory flare. "A physician-supervised weight loss program" is clean.
  "Boost your testosterone" becomes "hormone optimization under a licensed
  provider." We keep the claims medical-neutral and let the provider's judgment
  carry the promise.
- **HIPAA-clean by construction.** The site collects name and contact only.
  Zero medical history, zero symptoms, zero PHI through a web form. All of that
  lives inside their compliant intake/EHR platform, which we link to, never
  replace. If they want a symptom questionnaire, it runs on their HIPAA-covered
  tool, not our form. Say the phrase "HIPAA-clean" and mean it.
- **Telehealth state lines.** Many of these clinics can only treat patients in
  states where the provider is licensed. The site should say which states are
  served so out-of-state leads self-qualify instead of wasting a consult slot.
- **Standard disclaimer footer.** "Information is educational, not medical
  advice. Treatment requires a consultation and is at the provider's
  discretion." We build it in.
- I am not a compliance lawyer and I say so. I build to the known lines and flag
  anything gray for their medical director. That honesty is part of the pitch.

## Seasonality calendar (drives outreach + care-plan content)
- **Jan-Mar: the resolution surge.** GLP-1 and weight-loss demand peaks. New
  Year, new body. This is the single biggest window. A clinic without a working
  weight-loss page in January is bleeding.
- **Apr-Jun: summer-body run-up.** Weight loss stays hot. TRT and energy
  messaging climbs as men want to feel better for the active season.
- **Sep-Oct: post-summer reset.** Second weight-loss push. Longevity and
  hormone content lands.
- **Nov-Dec: slower, but memberships and gift-adjacent programs move.** Good
  window to build so January traffic hits a finished site.

## Their objections (expected) and our counters
1. "I get all my patients from Instagram / paid ads." -> "The ad finds them.
   Your site loses them. They tap the link and land on a page with no provider,
   no program, no price. You are paying for the click and dropping the catch."
2. "Hims and Ro are cheaper than me, I cannot compete on price." -> "You are not
   competing on price. You are competing on a real provider they can see and a
   local clinic they can call. Your site should show both. Right now it hides
   them, so you look like a worse version of Ro instead of the better version of
   local."
3. "Websites for medical stuff are a compliance minefield." -> "That is exactly
   why you want someone who knows the lines. No drug brand names, no outcome
   claims, no PHI through the form. I build these clean and I flag anything gray
   for your medical director. A template shop will get you in trouble."
4. "My EHR / telehealth platform already has a booking page." -> "Keep it. I do
   not replace your scheduler, I embed it. What I fix is everything before the
   booking: the reason they trust you enough to click it."
5. "We are already busy." -> "Busy with your best-margin programs? A site that
   sells the GLP-1 and TRT programs directly fills the recurring-revenue
   calendar, not just the one-off consults."
6. Price -> anchor to retained-patient value (above), then the one-or-two-
   patient payback line.
